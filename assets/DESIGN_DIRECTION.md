South Knoxville Ear

Visual Design Brief & Editorial Asset System

1. Core Ethos

The South Knoxville Ear should feel like a beloved neighborhood newspaper that somehow survived into the web era with all its personality intact. It is not sleek, corporate, minimalist, or startup-polished. It should feel local, handmade, funny, observant, and slightly mischievous.

The design voice is:

Retro community newspaper + campy local rumor sheet + hand-drawn Appalachian alt-weekly + South Knoxville porch gossip.

The publication should feel like it knows the neighborhood personally: Sevier Avenue, the Gay Street Bridge, the South Waterfront, the Urban Wilderness, Ijams, dogs, possums, fireflies, Pride events, coffee shop rumors, and odd civic updates. It reports real things, but with a wink.

The visual promise is:

We report it. You live it. We hear things.

⸻

2. Brand Mood

The brand should feel:

* Local, neighborly, and specific to South Knoxville
* Retro, but not fake-antique
* Campy, but not childish
* Editorial, but not stiff
* Handmade, but still polished
* Funny, but useful
* Nostalgic, but web-native

The design should make people feel like they found a local artifact: part newspaper, part event flyer, part handbill, part neighborhood bulletin board.

Avoid anything that feels too clean, techy, glossy, luxury, corporate, or generic “news website.”

⸻

3. Visual World

The site and related assets should consistently use:

* Warm cream newsprint backgrounds
* Black ink linework
* Stipple shading
* Vintage engraving textures
* Muted teal accents
* Rust-red emphasis marks
* Occasional golden-yellow highlights, especially for the Sunsphere mic
* Ornamental dividers, stars, rules, ribbons, and handbill-style borders
* Custom editorial spot illustrations for stories, sections, and cards

Everything should look like it belongs to the same local print universe.

The masthead is the source of truth. All future assets should feel like they could sit naturally underneath it.

⸻

4. Color Palette

Use a restrained print-inspired palette.

Base Colors

* Newsprint Cream: #F3E8D2
* Warm Paper Shadow: #E2D1B3
* Black Ink: #171512
* Faded Ink Gray: #4A4740

Accent Colors

* South Knoxville Teal: #7FAEA3
* Deep Bridge Green: #315D54
* Rust Red: #A94A34
* Dark Rust: #743124
* Sunsphere Gold: #D8A725
* Pale Blue Eye Accent: #7FB7D7

Use black and cream as the foundation. Teal and rust should be used as editorial accents, not dominant brand colors. Gold should be rare and special.

⸻

5. Background Treatment

The background should not be flat white. It should feel like aged but clean newsprint.

Use:

* Warm cream base
* Subtle paper grain
* Light uneven ink texture
* Faint halftone or fiber noise
* Slight edge darkening on large feature panels
* Boxed content areas with imperfect borders

The texture should be visible enough to create warmth, but not so heavy that it hurts readability.

⸻

6. Typography Direction

The type system should feel like old print but remain readable on the web.

Masthead / Display Type

Use distressed, vintage, woodtype-inspired lettering. This is appropriate for the logo and major hero headlines.

Qualities:

* Tall serif or slab-serif forms
* Engraved or letterpress texture
* High contrast
* Big theatrical scale
* Slightly irregular ink distress

Section Headers

Use condensed serif, slab serif, or bold vintage newspaper-style type. Section headers should feel like printed rubrics.

Examples:

* TOP STORIES & EVENTS
* WHAT WE’RE HEARING
* WEEKEND AUDIO BRIEFING
* MORE LOCAL STORIES

Body Copy

Use a readable serif that feels newspaper-adjacent. Body text should be warm and legible, not overly decorative.

Good general direction:

* Old-style serif
* Moderate contrast
* Slightly condensed if needed
* Comfortable line height

UI / Labels / Tags

Use small caps, condensed serif, or sturdy label-style lettering. Buttons and tags can feel like stamped print, ribbons, or small handbill labels.

Important production note: final website text should be live HTML/CSS whenever possible. Do not rely on generated images for important readable text, except in the masthead or decorative art.

⸻

7. Illustration Style

The illustration system is central to the brand.

Each article should receive a custom black-ink line drawing or stipple-print spot illustration before the article, before the image, or as the lead visual on story cards.

These should look like small editorial engravings from an old local paper.

Illustration Rules

* Use black ink linework as the base
* Use stipple dots and crosshatching for shading
* Use imperfect hand-drawn outlines
* Use cream negative space
* Add teal or rust accents sparingly
* Use gold only for Sunsphere-related details
* Keep drawings simple enough to work at small sizes
* Avoid photorealism
* Avoid slick vector icon style
* Avoid generic stock illustration

The icons should feel custom to the story, not like a generic icon pack.

⸻

8. Article Icon System

Every article card should begin with a custom editorial spot illustration. These should act like visual “story stamps.”

Recommended format:

* Square or vertical thumbnail frame
* Cream background
* Black stipple illustration
* Optional rust label or teal frame
* Small visual joke or local cue when appropriate

Examples:

Pride at Noon on Sevier Avenue
Line drawing of a rainbow parade flag hanging from an old streetlamp with a Sevier Ave sign.

Pride Afterglow at 6 PM
Disco ball, string lights, moon, or dancing shoes with small rust-red motion marks.

Weekend Audio Briefing
Vintage headphones, waveform, microphone, or Sunsphere-as-mic detail.

Rumor Mill / What We’re Hearing
Coffee percolator, whispering tin cans, a mailbox, or a tiny mill with smoke curling out.

South Waterfront Update
Bridge truss, riverbank, crane, dock, or boardwalk rail.

Urban Wilderness Fireflies
Mason jar with glowing fireflies, moon, trees, trail marker, or Ijams-inspired foliage.

Trolley / Old Sevier Route
Vintage trolley or bus with a hand-drawn route placard.

Paws of SoKno
Dog paw, leash, neighborhood dog, or tiny pet portrait.

Have a Tip? We’re All Ears
Mailbox, megaphone, ear, notepad, or anonymous envelope.

Each icon should be specific, charming, and slightly overdrawn in a good way.

⸻

9. Layout Principles

The website should behave like a modern homepage but look like a retro local newspaper.

Use:

* Clear masthead at the top
* Strong navigation bar
* Large feature article
* Audio module near the feature story
* Story-card grid
* Local calendar sidebar
* Lower “more local stories” section
* Tipline / newsletter module
* Footer with small icons, links, and bridge art

The site should feel dense and useful, but not chaotic. It should reward scanning.

Every page should include:

* A strong headline
* A custom illustration
* A short deck or intro
* A clear date/time/location when event-related
* A “Listen” or “Tip” pathway where relevant
* Decorative dividers that keep the old newspaper rhythm

⸻

10. Article Page Template

Each article page should follow this structure:

1. Section label
    Example: OLD SEVIER, RUMOR MILL, URBAN WILDERNESS, SOUTH WATERFRONT
2. Custom line drawing / stipple illustration
    Placed before the article image or at the top of the article header.
3. Headline
    Big, vintage editorial type.
4. Deck / summary
    One or two sentences explaining why the story matters.
5. Event facts or story metadata
    Date, time, place, cost, source, or “what we know.”
6. Main article image or image gallery
    Framed like a printed photo or clipping.
7. Article body
    Readable serif, generous line height, cream paper background.
8. Related local links
    “More from Old Sevier,” “More Urban Wilderness,” etc.
9. Audio option
    For suitable stories, include a small “Listen to this story” player.
10. Tipline box
    “Heard something? Send it to The Ear.”

⸻

11. Audio Design

Audio should feel like a recurring neighborhood radio dispatch.

The audio module should include:

* Title: “Weekend Audio Briefing” or “Listen to The Ear”
* Large circular play button
* Waveform graphic
* Time indicator
* Short description
* Subscribe links
* Optional vintage microphone or headphones line drawing

Tone:

Your 90-second look at what’s happening around SoKno.

The audio component should visually match the paper style, not feel like a modern embedded podcast widget dropped onto the page.

⸻

12. Asset Rules for Consistency

For every new story, create a matching editorial illustration using the same rules:

Prompt formula for custom graphics:

Create a small vintage newspaper spot illustration for a South Knoxville community article about [topic]. Use black ink linework, stipple shading, slight engraving texture, warm cream paper background, and restrained accents in muted teal and rust red. Make it feel like a hand-drawn local newspaper illustration, charming and specific, not generic. No modern glossy vector style.

For stronger local identity, add:

Include a subtle South Knoxville cue such as Gay Street Bridge ironwork, Sevier Avenue signage, riverfront railings, urban wilderness trail markers, fireflies, the Sunsphere, or neighborhood streetlamps.

For article cards, avoid putting critical text inside the illustration. Let the website handle text in live typography.

13. Web Implementation Notes

Use a consistent component system:

Core components

* Masthead
* Nav bar
* Feature story panel
* Audio briefing card
* Story card
* Mini article row
* Calendar item
* Tipline box
* Newsletter signup
* Footer banner

CSS direction

* Cream background with paper texture overlay
* Thin black borders with slight irregularity
* Teal section bars
* Rust-red labels and stars
* Serif typography
* Decorative horizontal rules
* Subtle halftone texture on image frames
* Slightly distressed buttons and ribbons

Important: keep the design charming but accessible. Body text still needs strong contrast and readable sizing.

⸻

14. Do / Don’t

Do

* Use custom local illustrations
* Make every story feel hand-labeled
* Keep the palette restrained
* Use texture, stipple, borders, and old print rhythm
* Keep event information clear
* Treat audio as a core format, not an afterthought
* Keep the tone playful but useful

Don’t

* Use generic stock icons
* Use glossy gradients or modern SaaS UI styling
* Use flat white backgrounds
* Overuse bright colors
* Make the site look like a corporate news template
* Put important article text inside generated images
* Let the retro style interfere with readability
* Use too many fonts

⸻

15. Editorial Personality

The writing and visuals should share the same personality.

Voice should be:

* Neighborly
* Curious
* Lightly funny
* Useful
* Locally fluent
* Mildly nosy, but never mean
* Clear about what is confirmed and what is rumor

Examples of house language:

* “What we’re hearing”
* “Around SoKno”
* “Weekend Watch”
* “We’re all ears”
* “The rumor mill”
* “Seen something? Heard something?”
* “Read by locals. Loved by locals.”
* “South Knoxville, all the way.”

The site should make people want to check it before the weekend, send in tips, listen to short audio updates, and share local stories.

⸻

16. North Star

Every design choice should answer this question:

Does this feel like a weird, wonderful, useful little South Knoxville newspaper that could only exist here?

If yes, it belongs.

If it feels generic, polished, corporate, or place-less, simplify it and make it more local, more hand-drawn, and more newspaper-like.
