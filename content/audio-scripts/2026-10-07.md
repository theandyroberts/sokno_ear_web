## Script (TTS-ready)

A grocery store, with onions in it, is the biggest news on Severe Avenue this week, and I mean that as a compliment. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Old Severe Market opens Saturday. It's the neighborhood grocery the district has been counting down to all month, on Severe Avenue, with produce islands, dairy and frozen, baking supplies, school supplies, and local honey. The listed hours are seven in the morning to seven at night. And for the first month, fifteen percent of every purchase goes toward food for people in Knoxville who need it. A hundred-dollar grocery run funds fifteen dollars of donated food.

Sunday is a free festival on the lawn at Kerns. Scruffy Roots Fest is W D V X's celebration of East Tennessee Americana. Gates open at noon, the music runs from one to seven thirty, and there are six acts: Crawdaddy Jones, Free Rein Bluegrass, Lazarus Lake, EmiSunshine, Big Pink, and The Dirty Gospel. Bring a lawn chair. The food, the drinks and the vendors are all on the property. There's a forty percent chance of a shower, so pack a rain layer.

Saturday at Printshop Beer Company on Island Home Avenue is Knox-Ill, the brewery's live spray-painting day. Artists paint from ten in the morning to ten at night, and a fish fry starts at three. Then on Sunday, Printshop has a vegan barbecue pop-up from one to four: Southern Fried Vegan, with fried chickun, smoked meats, and mac and cheese.

Saturday morning is also the first Urban Wilderness Fifty K. It starts at seven thirty at the Gateway Park at the end of James White Parkway: thirty-one miles through seven parks, including Imes Park, with close to four thousand feet of climbing. A ten K leaves the same line at nine. Registration closes Wednesday night, and packet pickup is Friday from four to eight at SouthSide Garage on Severe Avenue. If you're on the trails Saturday morning, expect runners.

Thursday night has two things on Severe Avenue. The 808, the music venue that opened last weekend, has its first sold-out show: Paul Oakenfold and The Crystal Method, doors at seven. If you don't have a ticket, know that the street will be full. Do not park in the Borderland Tees lot next door. You will be towed. And a couple of blocks up, Alliance Brewing has a DJ night. Music starts at eight.

Friday, Daniel Kimbro plays the outdoor stage at Trailhead Beer Market on Island Home Avenue at seven, with Stranger May. And Fly by Night, the seventies cocktail lounge, hosts a release show for the band Feral Kitty. Saturday afternoon at the Pink Cactus, the After Brunch Special puts six stand-up comics on at two thirty for five dollars. Saturday night, Fly by Night has a dance party with a new DJ, Dizzle Phunk. And Sunday at four, Two Heads Are Better Than One plays Trailhead.

Two things at Imes Park on Saturday. At eight, a naturalist leads the Big Day birding hike, two hours of counting fall migrants on the trails. Fifteen dollars, ages fourteen and up. And at ten, the free storytime at the Nature Playscape is all about flowers and plants. Parking there is five dollars a car.

Back at Kerns, there's a second mural going up, on the back of the old bakery. Anna Charney is painting Tennessee wildflowers: dogwood, trillium, milkweed, blue iris, passionflower and tulip poplar. And two big sculptures are already standing along the rail trail. Free to watch, any day.

The Vols are in Arkansas on Saturday at four fifteen, and Earl's on Waterfront Drive lists its game-day board for the day: two dollars off drafts and pizza, and buckets at six for the price of five. The rest of the week down there: ten-dollar puh-trone mango ritas Thursday, spicy pineapple ritas Friday, and seven-dollar mimosas on Sunday.

The weather: sunny and eighty-four Thursday and Friday, partly sunny and eighty-two Saturday with a slight chance of a shower after two, and seventy-nine on Sunday with that forty percent chance.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open, and I'll see you around So No!
