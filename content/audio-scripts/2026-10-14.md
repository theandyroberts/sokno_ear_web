## Script (TTS-ready)

Sweater weather arrives Friday night, and South Knoxville has planned the weekend around it. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

The big free thing is Sunday afternoon on the lawn at Kerns: the Fall Festival. At two, Cowtown Studios leads an hour of line dancing, no partner and no boots required. At three fifteen, School of Rock plays. All afternoon there's pumpkin painting, axe throwing, face painting and eleven local vendors, from cookies to vinyl records. Admission is free, the food hall behind the lawn is open nine to nine, and the forecast is sunny and sixty-eight.

Also Sunday, Hi-Wire on Barber Street holds Pup-oh-ween, its dog costume contest, from two to five. Check-in is two to two forty-five, the runway starts at three, and it's five dollars a dog, all of it to Small Breed Rescue of East Tennessee. Three categories: Spookiest Pooch, Most Creative Canine, and Dynamic Duo for a dog and owner in matching costumes. Watching is free, and there's a Halloween photo booth.

The 808, the new music venue on Severe Avenue, has two nights. Friday at seven, Goodbye June and Danny Worsnop play a blues and rock double bill. Saturday is All My Exes Live in Texas, a classic country night, doors at seven and the music at eight. Both are eighteen and up. There's a hot dog cart on the patio at every show now. And the parking note from the venue: do not park across from Trailhead Beer Market, and do not park in the Borderland Tees lot. Saturday's doors open right as the Alabama game ends at Nee-land, so expect the bridge to be slow at seven.

Fly by Night, the seventies cocktail lounge on Severe Avenue, has a Thursday dance night: Disco Demon Ahun plays disco and house from nine to midnight, five dollars at the door, twenty-one and up, with free devil horns for the first twenty people in. Then Saturday night, Nashville's Quiet Entertainer brings turntables and real-time remixing to the lounge as the pre-party for his new album, with Kaspar opening.

Saturday at the Pink Cactus, First Bass and Planet Doom stack ten DJs on two stages, from eight until one in the morning. Twenty dollars, eighteen and up, with vendors and a tie-dye station in the yard.

Thursday at six, a three-mile social run leaves Printshop Beer Company on Island Home Avenue, a mile and a half out and back on the greenway toward Imes Nature Center. Every pace is welcome. Park in the public lot under the bridge next to Mighty Mud, and come back to dollar wings on the patio.

At Trailhead Beer Market, Crawdaddy Jones plays the outdoor stage Friday at seven, a week after the Scruffy Roots stage at Kerns. Sunday at four, Greg Horne closes the weekend on the same patio. The kitchen, Gato Peligroso, has a new menu with a chile relleno smash burger and the jerk chicken back on.

Friday night at Kerns is the Ho'Down Social: Cowtown Studios runs line and country dancing from seven thirty to eleven thirty, with the bar on the lawn. It's the same crew that leads Sunday's lesson, so Friday is the long version.

Four things at Imes Nature Center. The Starlight Paddle goes out Friday and Saturday, seven to nine, a guided kayak trip on the river after dark: forty-five dollars, ages fourteen and up, kayaks and headlamps provided, and Saturday is the clear cold night. Saturday morning, the free storytime at the Nature Playscape is all about birds, ten to noon, with five-dollar parking. Sunday from one to three, a naturalist teaches a beginner workshop on mushrooms and lichen, fifteen dollars. And Sunday from two to five, the East Tennessee Bluegrass Association jam is at Meads Quarry, acoustic instruments only, free to play and free to listen.

On the trails Saturday, ScruffyCat and ScrappyKitty sends mountain bikers on a hundred-mile tour of every trail in Knoxville, with fifty- and twenty-five-mile versions, self-supported, from McClure Lane, with a bonfire at the end. Register at bikereg. And Saturday morning from nine thirty to noon, Catalyst Sports runs its adaptive mountain bike ride at Baker Creek; register to ride or to volunteer.

Saturday is Alabama at Tennessee, three thirty at Nee-land, and Earl's on Waterfront Drive lists its game-day board: two dollars off drafts and pizza, and beer buckets at six for the price of five. The rest of the week down there: ten-dollar puh-trone mango ritas Thursday, spicy pineapple ritas Friday, and seven-dollar mimosas on Sunday.

The weather: sunny and eighty-six Thursday, eighty on Friday with the cold front coming through that evening and a low of forty-six. Saturday is sunny and sixty-two with a night down to forty. Sunday is sunny and sixty-eight.

That's the weekend. Grab the jacket, keep your ears open, and I'll see you around Sohno!
