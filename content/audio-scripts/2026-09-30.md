## Script (TTS-ready)

A bucket, a prom crown, and a lawn full of people who know exactly what's about to happen. That's Friday night in South Knoxville. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Friday is the last outdoor movie of the season at Imes Park, and they saved the right one for October. It's the original Carrie, from nineteen seventy-six, which makes it fifty years old this fall. Doors open at six on the Visitor Center lawn, and the movie starts at dark, around seven thirty. Tickets are twelve dollars. There are food trucks and the beer garden, so leave the cooler at home. Bring a chair or a blanket. There's a thirty percent chance of a shower Friday evening, so check the event page before you load the car.

Also Friday night, Imes Park puts kayaks on the Tennessee River for a starlight paddle. Last week's paddle had a full moon. This one has none. The moon doesn't come up until eleven forty-one, so once the sun sets the river is as dark as it gets this close to town. Seven to nine, from the River Landing on Island Home Pike. Forty-five dollars covers the kayak, the life vest and a headlamp. Ages fourteen and up. The forecast is mostly cloudy, and if the clouds win, the same paddle runs three more times this month.

Next time you're at Kerns, look up. The tower on the old bakery now has fishing lures painted on it, several stories tall, with shadows that make them look like they're hanging off the brick. The mural is called Shiny Objects, by an artist named Naomi Haverland. It's part of the So No Rail Trail and Art Walk, close to four miles of trail from Chapman Highway to Imes Park, scheduled to open this fall. Legacy Parks says more murals and sculptures go in over the next few weeks, and after that, a painted train engine and ten-foot letters that spell So No.

Saturday morning, the Appalachian Mountain Bike Club holds its monthly work party at Baker Creek Preserve on Taylor Road. Nine to noon, and you don't have to ride a bike or sign up. They bring the tools. You bring gloves, sturdy shoes, safety glasses and water. At noon the sponsors buy lunch. Families with kids are welcome. There's a fifty percent chance of showers, so dress for mud.

Saturday at Imes Park belongs to the kids. At nine thirty there's a wildflower program for ages six to twelve, ten dollars a child, and the adult who comes along is free. Storytime at the Nature Playscape, ten to noon, is free as always, and this week it's all frogs and toads. For the grown-ups, a naturalist leads a two-hour moss walk at Mead's Quarry at one o'clock. Fifteen dollars, ages sixteen and up, and it's the last one on the calendar.

Auburn comes to town Saturday at three thirty, and two places on our side of the river have something on for it. Puckers, the sports grill on Mimosa Avenue, serves brunch nine to two with every screen on, so you can eat first and already have your seat at kickoff. And Earl's, down on Waterfront Drive, runs its game-day board all day, eleven to ten, with two dollars off pizza and drafts.

Sunday is the rain day. Showers are likely, and Imes Park has a morning that mostly doesn't care. At eight, the bird-banding team at Mead's Quarry catches, bands and releases songbirds while you watch. Five dollars, parking included, and it's the last banding morning on the calendar, so check the event page if it's pouring. At ten, there's a sound bath inside the Visitor Center. Singing bowls and a gong for an hour, twenty-five dollars, bring a mat. And at eleven thirty, Sunday yoga meets on the back patio, ten dollars, and moves inside if it rains.

One more, for Thursday. It'll be sunny and eighty-nine, the warmest day of the weekend, and the hooping class at Imes Park is indoors. Six o'clock in the Miller Building, twenty-five dollars, adults, hoops to borrow.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open, and I'll see you around So No!
