## Script (TTS-ready)

Friday the thirteenth lands on Severe Avenue this week, and the neighborhood is leaning into it. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Start with the big free one. Friday from six to nine, the Fall Asian Night Market takes over Hi-Wire's taproom on Barber Street. The Knox A A P I Business Association puts it together and The Thread T N magazine presents it. Two food trucks, Happy Song Pocha and Gohan Japanese Grill, park outside; inside, nine food makers and ten artists and shops set up, including A Lucky Little Bakery's milk bread and Korean salt bread, Ajumom's small-batch kimchi, and Sasa Starcake's little ceramic critters. It's free to walk in, all ages, and indoors, which matters, because Friday is cloudy and forty-eight and falling to thirty-two overnight. Parking is tight around the building, so carpool; the architects next door at Johnson Architecture are waiving their lot fee for the night.

Thursday night at Fly by Night, the seventies cocktail lounge on Severe Avenue, the goth night BellTower books a live band for the first time. Feyleux, the North Carolina darkwave duo, plays its new album Shadeweaver, out last week, with D J sets by Darkness and Cemetery Sex before and after. Doors at eight forty-five, music at nine, ten dollars, twenty-one and up. It's a rainy Thursday, so a dark lounge is about right.

Friday the thirteenth at The Pink Cactus, the karaoke-room bar with the big front yard, it's two things in one building. From four it's Alt Mart, the bar's first market of what it calls non-mainstream goods: art, flash tattoos, plants, potions, games. At six they crown M X Knoxville Goth, a pageant hosted by Purrahna Rivers, with live entertainment around it, and it's all ages until nine. Then at nine the same building turns into Aero Dream, a new Y two K dance night from D J Freake with Captain Midnight and Nocturne: flip phones, shiny C Ds, metallic tracksuits encouraged. Nine till one, ten dollars, eighteen and up.

The 808, the music venue at the top of Severe Avenue, has three nights in a row. Thursday it's Ahee's Bass Quest Tour, doors at eight, show at nine, eighteen and up. Friday it's the touring band Big Something, doors at seven, show at eight, eighteen and up. And Saturday it's SHOGUN releasing its new L P, Agonize in Defeat, and that one is all ages, doors at six, show at seven, well after the Vols finish at Texas A and M. Tickets for all three are on the venue's page, and our parking map is on the site.

Saturday afternoon, back at The Pink Cactus, The Comedy Sphere runs its After Brunch Special, stand-up at two thirty with doors at two. The booked headliner dropped, so the club's owner, Justin Chavez, who usually hosts, headlines the show for the first time. Tickets on the Comedy Sphere's site.

Two things at Kerns. Thursday from seven to nine, Alchemy Cigar Lounge, the downtown lounge, takes over Up Top, the rooftop bar, for a night called Sweaters, Flannels and Bourbon Barrels. Forty dollars gets you a cocktail, a Crowned Heads cigar and a guided tasting of Company Distilling whiskey, and the dress code is in the name. It's a rainy night, so plan on the covered side of the roof. And Sunday morning, seven to ten, Cars and Coffee starts up at Eggspectation, the breakfast place on the Kerns lot, with free drip coffee at the bar inside. It's the first one, and Kerns says it's every third Sunday from here on. Thirty-three degrees and sunny at seven, which is the weather car people want.

Four things at Imes Nature Center, and they're all small-group classes, so register on the park's page. Friday at five thirty, The Forest Speaks is beginner nature journaling, an hour and a half outside with the pens and paper provided, fifteen dollars. Saturday at two thirty, Carve and Create teaches beginners to carve and hand-press a block print, and you can bring an old shirt to print on, eighteen dollars. Sunday at nine, Fall Fungi I D walks beginners through mushrooms and lichens for two hours, fifteen dollars. And Sunday from two to five, the East Tennessee Bluegrass Association's monthly jam sits in a circle on the plaza by the visitor center. Bring any acoustic bluegrass instrument, no harmonicas or spoons, their rule. Beginners get the first hour, then it opens up to everybody. It's free, listeners are welcome, and Sunday is sunny and fifty-five.

Earl's on Waterfront Drive runs a different deal every day, and Saturday the game-day board comes out for Texas A and M at three: two dollars off drafts, six-dollar Orange Bliss cocktails, six beer buckets for the price of five, and two dollars off pizza, all day, with eight-dollar house margaritas on top. Thursday is ten-dollar puh-trone mango ritas, Friday nine-dollar spicy pineapple ritas, and Sunday seven-dollar mimosas and Bloody Marys.

One piece of neighborhood news. Legacy Parks says two more murals are being painted right now along the Sohno Rail Trail and Art Walk, the old rail line it's turning into a trail from Chapman Highway to Imes Nature Center. Whitney Herrington is painting herons and oversized dogwood blooms at Landing Health, and the New York artist Key Detail is painting a mountain dulcimer and a marble eagle on the Trimble building. That makes five murals and two sculptures along the walk, thirty-seven new native trees behind Rivers Edge, and the tunnel is getting new lights.

That's the weekend. Eat at the market, wear the tracksuit, bring the banjo, and I'll see you around Sohno!
