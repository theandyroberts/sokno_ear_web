## Script (TTS-ready)

Thursday night, if the clouds cooperate, you can stand in your own driveway and watch the moon disappear. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Here's the timing, because it matters. The moon is full Thursday night. At twenty-three past nine, the edge of the Earth's shadow touches it — soft, easy to miss. At ten thirty-three the dark shadow arrives, and after that you can't miss it: a bite out of the side that keeps growing, and the covered part going the color of a penny. Maximum is at twelve twelve Friday morning, with ninety-three percent of the moon swallowed and one thin bright rind left along the edge. It's forty degrees up in the south-southeast, which is high enough that you don't need a hill or a clearing or a boat. No glasses, no filter — a lunar eclipse is the safe kind. Binoculars help. The forecast right now says mostly cloudy with light showers, so no promises. But it costs you five minutes on the porch, and the next one anything like it is a year and a half out.

And earlier that same evening, there's opera in a quarry. Knoxville Opera closes its summer series at Augusta Quarry — that's inside Fort Dickerson Park, off Chapman Highway, the swimming-hole quarry with the green water. Seven o'clock, one hour, listed at five dollars, and it's built for all ages. Pirates and heroes and songs you already half know. It's done by eight, which leaves you plenty of time to get home and look up.

Now, Imes Park has read the same calendar. Friday night, Movies Under the Stars puts Napoleon Dynamite on the Visitor Center lawn — doors at seven, movie when it's actually dark, around a quarter to nine. Twelve dollars, ten if you're a member. Food trucks and the beer garden are open, dogs are welcome on a leash, and no coolers. Saturday morning at ten, storytime at the Nature Playscape out at Mead's Quarry — two hours, free, somebody reading out loud under the trees. Saturday at seven thirty, stand-up comedy and poetry on the back patio. And at eight thirty Saturday, a guided Full Moon Hike, ages ten and up, blacklights and headlamps provided. That one still had room Tuesday. The full moon paddles both nights are sold out, so if that's your thing, mark the September one now.

Then Sunday, the park goes easy: yoga at eleven thirty, and at two, a three-hour workshop setting dried flowers in resin and making jewelry out of them.

Over at Kerns, two things that have nothing to do with each other. Thursday, seven to nine, there's a bourbon tasting and cigar pairing up on the rooftop — forty dollars, and the dress code is sundresses and seersucker. Then Saturday morning, eight thirty, goat yoga. Thirty-five dollars for adults, twenty-five for kids five to twelve. The goats walk across your mat and the serious part of the class is over in about four minutes.

On Old Severe, Thursday does all the work. Hi-Wire on Barber Street runs pint night on top of an all-day happy hour. Trailhead Beer Market over on Island Home has free trivia at seven. After that the corridor has nothing scheduled at all — Alliance Brewing is open till ten Friday and Saturday, SouthSide Garage has the food trucks going, and that's the whole appeal.

One bit of neighborhood news. The brunch room off Blount Avenue opened Wednesday under a new name. Mimosas served its last day Tuesday, and the same building is now Puckers Sports Grill — the Gatlinburg place, brought over the mountain by the same family that ran Mimosas. The brunch dishes stayed on the menu. This is its first weekend, and it's the last quiet one before Tennessee's home opener on September fifth.

And down at the water, Earl's runs a different deal every day: ten-dollar puh-trone ritas Thursday, nine-dollar spicy ritas Friday, eight-dollar margs all day Saturday, seven-dollar mimosas and Bloody Marys all day Sunday. Tables face open water — and Thursday night, so does the sky.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open — and I'll see you around So-No!
