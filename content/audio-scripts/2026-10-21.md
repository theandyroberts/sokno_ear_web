## Script (TTS-ready)

The costumes come out a week early this year: Severe Avenue closes to cars Friday evening so the kids can trick-or-treat it. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Friday from five to seven thirty, the Old Severe District holds Spooky Streets. Part of Severe Avenue is closed and pedestrian-only, the businesses up and down the avenue hand out candy, and in between there's a bounce house, face painting, crafts, games, a dance party with DJ L L, and more than a dozen local vendors. It's free. Redbud Kitchen takes half off a kids' meal with an adult entree during the event. Park once at either end of the district, the Riverwalk garage or the gravel lot under the bridge, and walk in. It's sunny and seventy-three, and it drops into the fifties after dark, so a layer under the costume.

Scruffy Roots Fest has a new date: this Sunday, one to seven thirty, on the lawn at Kerns, free. It's W D V X's celebration of East Tennessee Americana, and the lineup is on the hour: Texas Tony and the Tornado Ramblers at one, Free Rein Bluegrass at two, Lazarus Lake at three, EmiSunshine at four, Yer Flowers at five, and Big Pink from a quarter past six. Bring a lawn chair. Vendors, food and drinks are on site, and the food hall behind the lawn is open nine to nine.

The Borderland Playhouse, the little theater inside the T-shirt shop on Severe Avenue, opens John, by Annie Baker, on Thursday. Free Spirit Theatre is producing. A young couple spend a weekend at a Gettysburg bed-and-breakfast whose owner collects dolls; it's slow, strange and funny, and it runs about three hours. Thursday and Saturday at seven thirty, Friday at eight because of the street closure, and a Sunday matinee at two. Twenty dollars, seating first come, so book online and come early.

Then Sunday night at seven, the same room has a free one-night readers' theatre: Best if Used By, by Doug Brendel, directed by Marty Ragsdale, with Chevy Anz and Carol Goans reading. No ticket.

Friday at nine, the Pink Cactus gives a whole night to drum and bass. It's the Big Pink House Party, volume five, with Andrew Fetch, Blindside and Bounce Back. Ten dollars online, fifteen at the door, twenty-one and up, and the doors open an hour and a half after Spooky Streets ends on the same avenue.

Fly by Night, the seventies cocktail lounge on Severe Avenue, has two nights. Thursday, Captain Ed and FreQ-K play industrial music; the lounge's own word for it is slammers. And Saturday night it's a vampire party for Knoxville's lesbian and sapphic community: drinks and mingling, a dress code of fangs and capes, and DJ Iris at ten. The creepy cocktail list is on all month.

At Trailhead Beer Market on Island Home Avenue, Rodney Lee and Corey Kimbro play the patio Friday at seven, and the Lonetones, Knoxville's mountain indie folk-pop band, close the weekend there Sunday at four. Both are outdoors and free, and the kitchen has a new Southern Hospitality burger with pimento cheese and candied bacon.

Thursday morning, nine to noon, Legacy Parks and Trees Knoxville are planting thirty-seven native trees along the new Sohno Rail Trail, at the River's Edge Apartments, seventeen-oh-one Island Home Avenue. Registration is free on the Trees Knoxville site and there were about thirty spots left Monday. Bring gloves.

Also Thursday, five thirty to seven thirty at Kerns, kids in costume sew their own stuffed monsters with Monsters Made with Love. Fifty dollars a child, with a treat bag, a costume contest for Dollywood passes, and every dollar to the Restoration House, which houses single mothers and their kids. Tickets are at the link in Kerns' bio.

Saturday the Vols are at South Carolina, kickoff at three thirty, and Printshop Beer Company takes a dollar off pints from three until the final whistle, with the game on the big screen and the patio open on the river. Friday there it's twelve-dollar flights all day.

Four things at Imes Nature Center. The big one is the Enchanted Forest, Saturday from three to eight at Meads Quarry: come in costume and walk the woods meeting the park's animal ambassadors and life-size puppets of native critters, with candy to take home. Timed entry, ten dollars a person, thirty for a group of four. Friday night from seven to nine is Mischief Night, a guided night hike with the nocturnal animals up close, fifteen dollars, ages six and up. Sunday at six thirty is the Full Moon Paddle under the Hunter Moon, two guided hours on the river with the kayak and the headlamp provided, forty-five dollars, ages fourteen and up, and it's the last paddle of the season; a full-moon hike leaves the visitor center at the same time for fifteen. And Sunday at two, the Crossword Play: a one-woman show where a crossword gets built in front of you, ninety minutes, register on the park's calendar.

Earl's on Waterfront Drive runs a different deal every day: ten-dollar puh-trone mango ritas Thursday, spicy pineapple ritas Friday, eight-dollar house margaritas Saturday with the game-day board, two dollars off drafts and pizza and buckets at six for the price of five, and seven-dollar mimosas Sunday while the Titans host the Browns at one.

That's the weekend. Keep the candy bucket by the door, and I'll see you around Sohno!
