## Script (TTS-ready)

There's one more brunch on Blount Avenue, and then that room becomes something else. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Mimosas serves its last Saturday and Sunday this weekend. Brunch runs nine to three both days, and the room stays open past it — until ten Saturday, until nine Sunday. Tuesday the twenty-fifth is the last day under that name; Wednesday it reopens as Puckers Sports Grill, which is the Gatlinburg place coming over the mountain. The brunch dishes are staying on the new menu — but if you've been meaning to go while it's Mimosas, this is the weekend.

Now, the good news, and there's a lot of it. Kerns has three nights of music booked and is charging for none of it. Friday at six, Scruffy City Soul Machine — that's the local soul and funk outfit, Aretha and Stevie and Otis, horns and all. At eight the floor turns over to the Ho'Down Social, line and country dancing, free, every skill level, no dress code. Then Saturday and Sunday, School of Rock brings more than a hundred and fifty kids to play across two full days — starting eleven-thirty Saturday morning, running through seven Sunday evening, and that's free too.

Here's my favorite thing this week. Imes Park is running a summer camp for adults. It's called Re-Discovery Camp, it's eighteen and over, and it goes Friday evening through Sunday. Caving, kayaking, naturalist hikes, animal encounters, arts and crafts, a campfire with s'mores — and Saturday night you sleep in a tent on the grounds. Three hundred dollars for the whole weekend, two-ten if you just come Saturday and Sunday. And if that's more weekend than you've got, the park's free Saturday storytelling runs ten to noon at the playscape, and Self Care Sunday yoga starts eleven-thirty.

Over on Old Severe, a quiet, generous stretch of days. Thursday, Fly by Night hosts Pages and Pours from five-thirty to nine — bring whatever you're reading. Hi-Wire on Barber Street does four-dollar drafts that same night, yoga at eleven both weekend mornings, and then the weekend's best idea: Saturday two to six, a grown-up book fair with adoptable pets in the room. Fair warning, you may go home with more than you planned. Sunday at two, they turn the taproom into a stained glass workshop.

And with the students back this week, two more worth knowing: SouthSide Garage, the food truck park on Severe Avenue, and Trailhead Beer Market over on Island Home — both open all weekend, nothing scheduled at either, which is the whole appeal.

And if none of that lands, there's the Pink Cactus — the big pink house on Severe Avenue with private karaoke rooms upstairs, booked by the hour. Nothing scheduled, no ticket, no crowd to sing in front of; you just go. All ages until nine, twenty-one and up after that. Park free down at Suttree Landing and walk the block and a half up.

One piece of neighborhood business. Last Thursday the Planning Commission approved the plan for the old Kerbela Shriners site — that's the five and a half acres where Severe Avenue meets Dawson. It's two hundred twenty-five apartments, a fifty-five-and-over community. The version that passed includes a public plaza at the corner built to hold food trucks, a public walkway east to west across the property toward the waterfront, and a piece of the land going to the city. Nothing changes on the ground yet — design and permitting come next.

And down at the water, Earl's runs a different deal every day: ten-dollar Pa-trone ritas Thursday, nine-dollar spicy ritas Friday, eight-dollar margs all day Saturday, seven-dollar mimosas and Bloody Marys all day Sunday.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open — and I'll see you around So-No!
