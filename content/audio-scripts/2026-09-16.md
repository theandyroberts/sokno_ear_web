## Script (TTS-ready)

Saturday runs from ten in the morning to nine at night around here, and you are going to have to make some choices. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Start with the free one. Marble Springs, the old governor's farm out on West Governor John Severe Highway, throws John Severe a birthday party every September, and Saturday he turns two hundred and eighty-one. Eleven to four, costs nothing. What makes it good is that the craftspeople actually work: a blacksmith at a live forge, somebody cooking over an open hearth, a broom being tied by hand, a weaver, a potter. The Knoxville Irish Step Dancers go on at twelve thirty, a band called the Bearded Trio plays an hour at one fifteen, and there's a lecture on the Overmountain Men at two thirty. It's going to be ninety-four degrees, so go early and take water.

Then Saturday night, there's a circus on the lawn at Imes Park. Acrobats, aerials and silks, jugglers, and fire performers — and the fire is the back half, because the show is built around the light going. Gate and marketplace open at six, the show runs seven thirty to nine with an intermission. Twenty-two dollars for an adult, fifteen for a kid six to twelve, under six free, and your parking is in the ticket. There's no seating, so bring a chair or a blanket. Food trucks and the beer garden are open from six, which means the smart move is to get there early and eat on the grass. One clock warning: the Vols kick off at Nee-land at seven forty-five, fifteen minutes after the show starts. That's a choice you only get to make once.

Friday belongs to a different lawn. Imes Park and Central Cinema have been running movies outdoors since May, and Friday is Lord of the Rings. Gate at seven, movie starts when it gets dark, about eight fifteen. Twelve dollars, less if you're a member of either. Food trucks and the beer garden handle dinner. Bring a chair. Dogs are welcome on a leash. No coolers, no outside alcohol — they're firm about that one. And buy ahead, because they don't promise walk-ups.

Saturday morning, before any of it, storytime at the Nature Playscape out by Mead's Quarry has something extra. It's free every Saturday, ten to noon, somebody reads a book out loud on the blankets. This week the somebody is the author — Mona Martin Batchelor, reading from a children's book of hers called Nora T and the Champion Tree that isn't published yet. Under real trees. That's a nice piece of luck.

Sunday is the quiet day and Imes Park fills it end to end. Bird banding at eight in the morning, five dollars, and that five includes your parking — you meet at Mead's Quarry and walk down to the banding table. Painting by the pond at eleven. Yoga at eleven thirty. A beginner mushroom workshop at twelve thirty. And at two, the East Tennessee Bluegrass Association jam, which is free, runs till five, and welcomes any acoustic instrument that isn't a harmonica or a spoon. You don't have to play. You can just sit there.

And if Sunday outdoors at ninety-three degrees is not your idea of a weekend, Puckers, the sports grill on Mimosa Avenue, runs brunch nine to two Saturday and Sunday with every screen on. This week that means the one o'clock Titans game lands inside the last hour of the pancakes. It is the one thing on this list that is indoors and air-conditioned.

Down at the water, Earl's on Waterfront Drive has a different deal every day, eleven to ten — puh-trone mango ritas Thursday, spicy pineapple Friday, house margaritas Saturday, mimosas and Bloody Marys Sunday. Saturday they've added a second board for the game.

And the weather, because it matters to almost everything on this list: ninety-three to ninety-five all four days, with just a slight chance of a storm Friday and Sunday. It is hot, not wet. Plan for shade, not for rain.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open — and I'll see you around So No!
