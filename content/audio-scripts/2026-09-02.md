## Script (TTS-ready)

Whatever you had planned for Saturday afternoon, the University has other ideas. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Let's start with the thing that touches everybody. Tennessee plays its first home game of the season Saturday at three thirty, against Furman. A hundred thousand people are coming to a stadium you can see from the end of half a dozen So-No streets — and the useful thing from over here is that you don't have to drive into any of it.

KAT runs a game-day shuttle out of Krutch Park, on Clinch Avenue downtown. Ten dollars round trip, bought in the Transit app, and no parking fee, because you didn't park. Buses start three hours before kickoff — so about twelve thirty — and run until game time, dropping you at Walters Life Sciences on campus. Coming back, you board at Walters from the start of the fourth quarter until about an hour after it's over. And from the north end of the neighborhood you can walk to it: over the Gay Street bridge, up Gay Street to Clinch, and the park is right there. If you'd rather drive, the Civic Coliseum park and ride is thirty dollars a vehicle including the ride, and those garages open at noon. One warning — the Old City shuttle only runs for the big conference opponents, so it is not an option Saturday.

Now, about this redesigned traffic plan you've been hearing about. Be a little skeptical of how new it is. Nee-land Drive running three lanes west and one lane east is not a change — those eastbound lanes were typically sitting empty, which is why it's set up that way, and one lane going east will be plenty Saturday. The genuinely new piece is smaller: that far-left westbound lane now runs as a Kingston Pike Express Lane. If you're headed for the near west side after the game, it might be worth a try. Gates also open a half hour earlier than they used to.

So here's the practical version. Saturday morning is completely normal — that's your window for anything across the river. Traffic builds through midday, the team walks in around a quarter past one, kickoff's at three thirty, and the game ends near a quarter to seven. By eight it's over and the roads hand themselves back.

One more thing about Saturday, and it's not small: it is going to be dangerously hot. Near ninety-seven Thursday, ninety-six Friday, ninety-five at kickoff Saturday, with the heat index running higher than that. The Weather Service is already telling people to take breaks and drink water. A three-thirty kickoff means the whole first half is played in the worst of the afternoon. Bring water, find shade, and check on the people who won't.

Now, the best thing on this weekend has nothing to do with football. Thursday night at eight, Imes Park takes a group out on the trails to look for bats. A naturalist does the talking, you stop at blacklight stations that light up an entire layer of the forest floor you can't see at noon, and out there on the property there's a bat house — ten feet by twelve, built to hold up to a hundred thousand Brazilian free-tailed bats, standing on ground that held industrial lime kilns until the nineteen-seventies. Fifteen dollars, ages ten and up, registration required, and expect a mile and a half over rocks and roots, so wear real shoes. One rule, and they mean it: never touch a bat.

Friday night, Imes Park puts All Dogs Go to Heaven on the Visitor Center lawn. Doors at seven, film around half past eight, twelve dollars, ten if you're a member. Food trucks and the beer garden are open, no coolers, and leashed dogs are welcome — which, for that particular film, is a choice.

Saturday morning at ten, there's free storytime at the Nature Playscape out by Mead's Quarry. Two hours, in the shade, costs nothing, and it's finished by noon — which this week is the whole point, because noon is when the roads start filling.

At Kerns, they've decided what they are on a Tennessee Saturday, and they've published the prices, which I appreciate. Three hundred dollars reserves a private section of the lawn for twenty people — a TV, couches, a canopy, and a parking spot. Twenty-five hundred takes the small rooftop for seventy-five. That's for the reunion or the office party, not for you and one other person. Walking into the hall is still free, and it's open eight in the morning till eleven at night.

And on Blount Avenue, Puckers gets its first one. The sports grill that took the old brunch room ten days ago calls itself, in its own words, your game-day headquarters. Saturday at three thirty is when that sentence gets tested. Their Knoxville hours still aren't posted, so call ahead.

Thursday belongs to Old Severe. Hi-Wire on Barber Street runs pint night on top of an all-day happy hour, doors at three, and on a ninety-seven degree day the enormous air-conditioned floor is a better bet than the roof deck until the sun's well down. And if you keep going straight past the new traffic circle, one more block, Trailhead Beer Market has free trivia at seven — no cover, no team required.

Down at the water, Earl's runs a different deal every day: ten-dollar puh-trone ritas Thursday, nine-dollar spicy ritas Friday, eight-dollar margs all day Saturday, seven-dollar mimosas and Bloody Marys all day Sunday. And because Monday's Labor Day, there's a fifth rung — eight-dollar house margaritas all day, after everybody's gone home.

That's the weekend. Drink water, ride the bus — and I'll see you around So-No!
