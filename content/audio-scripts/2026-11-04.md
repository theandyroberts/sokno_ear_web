## Script (TTS-ready)

The mountain bikers are coming home to Baker Creek this weekend, and the first Friday of November has the avenue busy. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

The big one is Fall Fest, the Appalachian Mountain Bike Club's seventeenth annual homecoming at Baker Creek Preserve, Friday and Saturday, and it's free. Friday from five there's a beer garden and food trucks, the Knoxville Utility Band plays bluegrass at five thirty, the pump track and the jump line are lit up at six thirty, there's a film at six forty-five and adventure slideshows under the stars at eight. Saturday is the full day: group rides for every speed from nine thirty, a bike parade at twelve thirty that anyone can join, kids' rides at one, the Whip Off at the bottom of Devil's Racetrack at three, where riders throw the biggest jumps in the Urban Wilderness, and after dark the Best Line Invitational on the lit jump line at seven, with five bands on the main stage and a silent disco to close. Park free at the Baker Creek Bottoms lot, South Doyle Middle School, or the Gateway Park lot under Severe-ville Pike, and stay off the neighborhood streets. Friday's partly sunny and seventy, dropping to forty-five overnight; Saturday's mostly sunny and sixty-six, so bring a jacket for the night events.

Friday is First Friday on Severe Avenue. Latin dot Knox, the Latin house party, takes over Printshop at nine with three D Js: E V opens with the classics, Jason Davila out of Atlanta takes the peak, and Minitiger closes. Twenty dollars, twenty-one and up, at fifteen thirty-two Island Home Avenue. Printshop's twelve-dollar flights run noon to nine if you want to get there early.

Also Friday, the 808, the music venue at eight-oh-eight Severe Avenue, gives the night to the locals. College Night is three Knoxville bands on the hour: Ella Pinchock at eight, Fort Knox at nine, Pocket Ace at ten. Doors at seven, eighteen and up, five dollars at the door or free if you R S V P on the venue's page.

And on Barber Street, Sohno Sourdough opens up for First Friday with artist Peter Riesing's work on the walls and Paradise Chocolate at a table, pouring samples of a new chocolate tonic alongside its bars. Two small South Knoxville makers in one room.

Saturday night at Fly by Night, the seventies cocktail lounge on Severe Avenue, it's Cut Corners, the Atlanta collective, with what they call bouncy techno, banging club and bubbling house, nine till one, ten dollars. The Vols kick off against Kentucky at six fifteen across the river, so the game is about over when the first D J goes on.

Sunday afternoon at Printshop, it's Bow Wow Bingo, two to four. Knox Vintage Pet Project, the rescue for senior dogs and the ones with medical needs, sells the bingo cards and brings the foster dogs. Kid friendly, dog friendly, beer friendly, their words, and it's mostly sunny and sixty-five for the patio.

Three things at Kerns. Thursday at seven, Up Top, the rooftop bar, runs its first karaoke night, seven to nine, with half off select drinks and a date night up for grabs. Same night at seven, All Play Live turns the lawn into a family game show, parents and kids as a team playing for prizes; showers are likely Thursday, so check their feed before you go. And Saturday morning at eight thirty, goat yoga with Misfit Herd comes to the lawn. The November first class was cancelled, this one's on, tickets on their page, and it's forty-two degrees at dawn, so dress for a cold mat.

Four things at Imes Nature Center. Saturday from nine to noon, Wilderness Basics teaches shelter, fire and finding food, fifteen dollars, ages fourteen and up. Saturday at two, Moss Meander walks Mead's Quarry with a naturalist and a hand lens, two hours, fifteen dollars, sixteen and up. Sunday at eight in the morning, the bird banding station behind Meads Quarry opens to the public: songbirds netted, banded and released in gloved hands, five dollars, ninety minutes. And Sunday at ten, a sound bath with Tibetan and crystal singing bowls fills the big room at the visitor center, twenty-five dollars; bring a mat and a blanket.

Earl's on Waterfront Drive runs a different deal every day, and Saturday the game-day board comes out for Kentucky: two dollars off drafts, six-dollar Orange Bliss cocktails, six beer buckets for the price of five, and two dollars off pizza, all day, with eight-dollar house margaritas on top. Thursday is ten-dollar puh-trone mango ritas, Friday nine-dollar spicy pineapple ritas, and Sunday seven-dollar mimosas and Bloody Marys.

One piece of neighborhood business. In August we told you the Planning Commission had approved two hundred twenty-five apartments for people fifty-five and over on the old Kerbela Shriners site, where Severe Avenue meets Dawson. That's off. On October thirteenth, City Council overturned the approval, five to four, on an appeal from a South Waterfront advocacy group. The developer can bring a new plan, walk away, or go to court, and hasn't said which. We'll be watching.

That's the weekend. Ride, sing, meet a goat, and I'll see you around Sohno!
