## Script (TTS-ready)

Shakespeare on the lawn and a World Cup final on the big screen — same weekend, same neighborhood. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Ear.

Start Thursday, when Knoxville Shakespeare opens at Eye-ams Park — and it opens twice. The Merry Wives of Windsor goes up Thursday night, King Henry V follows Friday, and the two trade nights all weekend long. Curtain is seven-thirty, doors at seven. Bring a lawn chair or a blanket, because the lawn is the seating. Parking's free starting thirty minutes before showtime, kids under fifteen get in free — and every Thursday show is pay-what-you-can. So opening night costs whatever you decide it costs.

Then Sunday at three, the World Cup ends. Spain is in the final; their opponent gets settled Wednesday. If you'd like to be there in person, tickets start at seven thousand, two hundred fifty-three dollars. Or you could walk to Kerns, where the big screen has been the neighborhood's tournament headquarters since the group stage.

Some news: the Severe Avenue rezoning is dead. The Planning Commission followed its staff and denied all three actions. And the two-hundred-twenty-unit apartment plan for the old Kerbela site didn't get a yes or a no — it got sent back to committee.

Saturday belongs to Disco Chicken, which turns one at Kerns with a gluten-free festival all day and a silent disco at nine. Knoxville's first all-gluten-free restaurant, ranked one of the best anywhere, right here — turning one.

And Friday is the fullest day of all: nine-dollar spicy pineapple margaritas at Earl's from four to six, which lets out ninety minutes before curtain. Or, if you'd rather be on the water, there's an evening paddle at seven-thirty — the same hour as Henry V. You'll have to choose.

That's the week. Keep your ears open — and I'll see you around So-No!
