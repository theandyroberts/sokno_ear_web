## Script (TTS-ready)

The last day Knoxville saw a high in the seventies was June twenty-third. That streak ends this weekend, and South Knoxville has plans for it. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

Start with Sunday, because Sunday is the big one. Channel eight sixty-five, the Knoxville DJ collective, takes over Mead's Quarry at Imes Park from noon until sundown, and it is free and all ages. Six DJs, the quarry wall on one side and the lawn on the other, the beer garden and the concession stand open all day, a food truck on the property. Sunset is seven twenty-five, so figure the music stops a little after seven. Bring a blanket and a layer for the last hour. It'll be seventy-eight with sun, and the temperature drops fast once the light is off the water.

Saturday night, the Harvest Moon comes up over the river at seven eighteen, and Imes Park has a paddle timed to it. Two hours on the Tennessee River from the River Landing on Island Home Pike, launching at seven thirty, eight minutes before sunset. Forty-five dollars, and that covers the kayak, the life vest and a headlamp. Bring your own paddle if you like, but not your own boat. These fill up, so book it ahead.

Saturday in the daytime, the park has a free art thing that only works on a sunny day, and Saturday is a sunny day. It's World Cyanotype Day, and a group called The Big Camera sets up on the plaza from noon to three. You lay a leaf on cloth that's been treated to react to light, the sun does the exposure, you rinse it in water, and you walk away with a white silhouette on deep blue. It costs nothing. They ask you to reserve a free ticket so they know how much cloth to bring.

Two more Saturday things at Imes Park for the kids. Storytime at the Nature Playscape, ten to noon, free as always, is reading a picture book about the moon, on the one day of the month when the real one comes up full that night. And at three, the park introduces its newest animal ambassador, which is an opossum. An hour with the animal, then nature journaling and a drawing to take home. Ten dollars, ages five and up.

Now, the football. Texas comes to Nee-land Saturday at noon, and three places on our side of the river have something on for it. Puckers, the sports grill on Mimosa Avenue, serves brunch nine to two with every screen on, which means the game starts in the middle of the pancakes. Get there early. Earl's, down on Waterfront Drive, opens its game-day board at eleven, so for once it covers lunch. And Kerns has started renting sections of its lawn as private tailgates. Three hundred dollars gets a group of twenty a third of the patio with a television, two couches and a canopy. You book it through a form on their site, so ask today.

Thursday is the wet day. Rain is likely most of the day, and the chance is down to fifty-fifty by evening. Two things at Imes Park go anyway. The last bat walk of the season leaves Mead's Quarry at eight, fifteen dollars, ages ten and up, with a naturalist calling species overhead and blacklight stations along the trail. Check the event page before you leave the house. And the one indoor thing on the park's Thursday is a hooping class in the Miller Building at six. Twenty-five dollars, adults, hoops to borrow.

Sunday morning, before the DJs, the park runs a two-hour session called Renew in Nature: an outdoor yoga flow, then a sound bath, then a slow guided walk in the trees. Ten to noon, forty dollars, bring a mat. And Sunday at three there's a beginner mushroom workshop and a fall wildflower walk, fifteen dollars each. The rain on Thursday and three dry days after is about the recipe for mushrooms coming up.

And the trails. The Urban Wilderness is a thousand acres and sixty miles of trail on our side of the river, and for three months the only usable hours have been the first two after sunrise. Friday, Saturday and Sunday are seventy-seven and sunny with nights in the low fifties. The whole day is yours. Friday morning will be muddy from the rain. Saturday it'll have set up. And if you want the trails to yourself, the noon kickoff clears them for three hours.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open, and I'll see you around So No!
