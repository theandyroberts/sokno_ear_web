## Script (TTS-ready)

Sunday night the trail behind the Imes Park Visitor Center gets strung with lights and lined with art, and it's the one night a year you need a ticket to walk it. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

That's Twilight at Imes, the park's biggest fundraiser of the year. Six to nine on Sunday, small bites from local kitchens, beer and wine, a campfire with a real s'mores bar, a band, a silent auction, and a run of art installations along the paved trail into the trees. A hundred and seventy-five dollars a ticket, two seventy-five if you want the cocktail hour at five. That money is what keeps the park free the other three hundred and sixty-four days, which is the whole argument. If you're not going, plan around it — the Visitor Center end of the park is a private party from late afternoon Sunday. The Mead's Quarry side is untouched.

And the park has a full weekend before that. Friday at five thirty, a naturalist takes a small group out to look at mushrooms — what they are, which parts are which, how to tell a fungus from a lichen. It's the first of a fall series, fifteen dollars, ages fourteen and up, and you register ahead. After a week of storms the logs should be busy.

Saturday morning at ten, storytime at the Nature Playscape out by Mead's Quarry starts its fall run — sixteen Saturdays from here, ten to noon, free. Somebody reads a story out loud on the blankets, and then it's a craft or it's the climbing logs, depending on the week. Bring a chair and a snack. Parking on that side is five dollars a car unless you're a member.

Now, football. Tennessee's first road game is Saturday at seven, at Georgia Tech, on ESPN — first time in Atlanta since nineteen eighty-six. And an away game is the night a sports grill is actually for. Puckers, on Mimosa Avenue, spent last Saturday watching the crowd walk past it to Nee-land. This Saturday the crowd stays put. They take reservations on Rezzy, and for a seven o'clock kickoff I'd make one. The weekday happy hour does not run Saturday, so don't count on the dollar lager.

Friday night, if you'd rather walk than drive, One Knox turns its stadium into a beer festival with a soccer match attached. KnoxtoberFest — gates at five, a polka band in the beer garden, a free stein for the first thousand fans over twenty-one, and beer is three dollars all match. Kickoff against Naples is at six minutes past seven. It's across the river in the Old City, but from the Severe Avenue end of the neighborhood that's the Gay Street bridge and a few blocks. A mile on foot. And the champs have drawn four in a row, so they could use the noise.

Thursday belongs to Old Severe, same as every week. Hi-Wire on Barber Street runs pint night on top of an all-day happy hour, and Trailhead Beer Market, one block past the traffic circle, has free trivia at seven.

Down at the water, Earl's keeps a different deal every day: ten-dollar puh-trone ritas Thursday, nine-dollar spicy ritas Friday, eight-dollar margs all day Saturday, seven-dollar mimosas and Bloody Marys all day Sunday.

One weather note. It's going to be hot — right around ninety all four days — with a coin-flip chance of an afternoon storm every one of them. Everything outdoors on this list is worth a rain plan.

And a look ahead: next Friday, Lord of the Rings plays on the Imes Park lawn, and next Saturday, Marble Springs throws Sevier Day — free, eleven to four, on this side of the river. More on both next week.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open — and I'll see you around So-No!
