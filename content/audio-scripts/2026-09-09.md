## Script (TTS-ready)

Sunday night the trail behind the Imes Park Visitor Center turns into a gallery — lights in the trees, art along the path — and it's the one night a year you need a ticket to walk it. Andy Roberts here with your South Knoxville Events and Rumors.

That's Twilight at Imes, the park's biggest fundraiser of the year. The installations are the point: pieces built along the paved Universal Trail, made to be walked through slowly after dark. Around them, small bites from local kitchens, beer and wine, a campfire with a real s'mores bar, a band, and a silent auction. Six to nine on Sunday. A hundred and seventy-five dollars a ticket, two seventy-five if you want the cocktail hour at five. That money is what keeps the park free the other three hundred and sixty-four days. If you're not going, plan around it — the Visitor Center end of the park is a private party from late afternoon Sunday. The Mead's Quarry side is untouched.

And the park has a full weekend before that. Friday at five thirty, a naturalist takes a small group out to look at mushrooms — what they are, which parts are which, how to tell a fungus from a lichen. It's the first of a fall series, fifteen dollars, ages fourteen and up, and you register ahead. After a week of storms the logs should be busy.

Saturday morning at ten, storytime at the Nature Playscape out by Mead's Quarry starts its fall run — sixteen Saturdays from here, ten to noon, free. Somebody reads a story out loud on the blankets, and then it's a craft or it's the climbing logs, depending on the week. Bring a chair and a snack. Parking on that side is five dollars a car unless you're a member.

Now, football. Tennessee's first road game is Saturday at seven, at Georgia Tech, on ESPN — first time in Atlanta since nineteen eighty-six. And an away game is the night a sports grill is actually for. Puckers, on Mimosa Avenue, spent last Saturday watching the crowd walk past it to Nee-land. This Saturday the crowd stays put. They take reservations on Rezzy, and for a seven o'clock kickoff I'd make one.

Up Chapman Highway, Kerns is one building with four bars in it, and this week I've mapped them. Monday Night Proofing Co. is the taproom — twenty taps, cocktails, its own door, and it stays open till midnight Friday and Saturday. UpTop is the rooftop. Peter's Market Bar is the one off the main hall. And Eggspectation, the breakfast place, keeps pouring cocktails till nine. The food deal this week is Roni's Mac Bar — download the app and five dollars comes off. For the game, Kerns rents lawn sections with their own television, three hundred dollars for twenty people, and the packages cover road games too. The hall is open till eleven Saturday, so the fourth quarter fits.

Thursday belongs to Old Severe, same as every week. Hi-Wire on Barber Street runs pint night on top of an all-day happy hour, and Trailhead Beer Market, one block past the traffic circle, has free trivia at seven.

One weather note. It's going to be hot — right around ninety all four days — with a coin-flip chance of an afternoon storm every one of them. Everything outdoors on this list is worth a rain plan.

And a look ahead: next Friday, Lord of the Rings plays on the Imes Park lawn, and next Saturday, Marble Springs throws Sevier Day — free, eleven to four, on this side of the river. More on both next week.

That's the weekend. Keep your ears open — and I'll see you around So No!
