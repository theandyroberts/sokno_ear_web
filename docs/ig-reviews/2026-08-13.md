The poster cron (`*/15`) is healthy and logging `0 posted, 0 failed, 0 due` — correctly, because nothing is approved. **The moment approval lands, E1's burst bug fires and three posts go out inside one 15-minute cron tick** (the two overdue plus episode-drop). That is the exact pattern that produced three of the account's worst-performing posts. E1 was never implemented, so nothing prevents it. I did not approve or post anything.

**2. Week 4 is set to post 9 — the largest week in the account's history — into the trend that says do the opposite.** Reach per post has fallen 16.1 → 9.9 → 8.3 as weekly volume ran 7 → 8 → 6. The staged No. 9 queue is nine posts to 49 followers. Baseline E2 recommended cutting to three. This is a 50% *increase* over last week, and it will be the fourth consecutive week of testing "more posts" against a follower base that has grown by three. The queue builder stages one post per story by default; nothing in the pipeline caps volume.

**3. Followers grew +3 during a six-day posting blackout.** Last post was Aug 7, 9:00 PM. Between then and this pull the account published nothing and gained three followers — proportionally its best growth stretch since launch (+6.5% in six days, against +46 in 52 days). n=3 and unattributed, so this is a signal, not a result. But it points the same way the baseline's post-attributed follows do: **zero of 49 followers have ever been attributed to a post.** The feed is not the growth engine. Whatever is producing followers — the site, the newsletter, the phone line, word of mouth — is off-platform, and the review can't see it from this API.

**4. None of E1–E5 shipped.** No commits touched `ig-queue.mjs`, `ig-post.mjs`, or `ig-approve.mjs` since the baseline. All five experiments remain staged and unsigned. This is expected two days on and is not a criticism — but it means the No. 9 week will run on exactly the configuration that produced the 16.1 → 8.3 decline, and will therefore produce a fourth week of data about a pipeline you're already planning to change. That's a wasted week of the only asset the account generates.

**5. The queue file is untracked server state, which hides drip failures from the repo.** `content/ig-queue/` is gitignored (deliberately — commit `3141ac7`, so the cron can mutate it without breaking `git pull`). The consequence is that the only place drip state is visible is the VPS. From a local checkout, a week where the queue was never staged looks identical to a week where it was staged and posted. Today that gap was closed by another session while this review was running; had it not been, the first visible symptom would have been a silent weekend with no posts.

## 3. Action plan

Ordered by deadline. **Items 1 and 2 need a decision today** — the No. 9 queue is live-but-unapproved and the weekend it covers starts tonight.

### A1 — Decide the No. 9 approval before this evening *(needs Andy, today)*
Three options, in order of my recommendation:

1. **Trim to 3–4, then approve.** Drop the two overdue items and the episode-drop card (worst content type on record, avg reach 7.5, no reshare path):
   ```bash
   node scripts/ig-approve.mjs 2026-08-13 --drop=hummingbird-weekend,call-the-ear,episode-drop,kerbela-hearing,hiwire-weekend
   ```
   Leaves mimosas-puckers (9 AM), specials-board (1 PM), kerns-allplay (Fri 7 PM), ijams-sunday (Sun 11 AM) — four posts, properly spaced, no burst, and it runs E2 for free on the week it matters.
2. **Approve as staged** — nine posts, accepting a three-post burst at the next cron tick.
3. **Hold the week** and ship E1 first.

Option 1 costs nothing, avoids the burst without a code change, and turns a week that was going to be wasted into the E2 test. But the hummingbird story is the week's lead and dropping it is a real editorial cost — that call is yours, not mine.

### A2 — Ship E1 (the burst guard) before the No. 10 queue *(needs sign-off)*
Unchanged from the baseline and now demonstrably urgent: one post per run, minimum ~4 hours apart, hard quiet window 10 PM–7 AM, overdue items rescheduled forward instead of dumped. This is the only one of the five experiments that fixes a *bug* rather than testing a hypothesis, and it would have made A1 a non-decision.
*Measures:* burst count → 0; story-banner reach vs. the 13.0 slotted baseline.

### A3 — Cap queue volume in `ig-queue.mjs` *(needs sign-off)*
Add a `MAX_POSTS_PER_WEEK` constant (start at 4) and have the builder stage only the highest-priority stories, marking the rest `status: "held"` rather than dropping them. Makes E2 the default instead of a manual `--drop` list every Thursday, and stops the pipeline from silently scaling volume with story count.
*Measures:* total weekly reach at 4 posts vs. 8.3 avg × 6 last week — the honest test is total, not per-post.

### A4 — Add a drip-state check to the publish path
Because the queue is invisible from the repo (Finding 5), the Wednesday publish check should assert that a queue exists for the new episode and report its approval state. A few lines in the existing publish routine, or a `--check` flag on `ig-queue.mjs`. Turns "the drip silently didn't run" from something a monthly review catches into something publish day catches.

### A5 — Get one number from off-platform *(needs Andy)*
Finding 3 is the only genuinely new signal in this review and the API cannot resolve it: `follower_count` and `profile_views` day-series both return empty on this token, and post-attributed follows are zero. If the site already logs referrer or the newsletter reports click-through, one number — where the last ten followers came from — would tell us whether the feed deserves any of this effort. Right now the plan is optimizing a channel that has never been shown to acquire anyone.
