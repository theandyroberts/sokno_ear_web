## 2. Findings

**1. The account is pre-distribution, not under-optimized.** Eight non-follower accounts reached in a month is the whole story. Instagram is not showing this content to anyone outside the follower list, and the follower list is 46 people. Every caption, timing, and hashtag lever below operates on a base too small to produce a readable signal — a good post and a bad post are separated by single-digit reach.

**2. Reach per post is falling as volume holds: 16.1 → 9.9 → 7.7.** This is the most alarming trend in the data, and the timing is diagnostic. Week one of the pipeline (Jul 22) was the strongest week the account has ever had — 7 posts, all reasonably spaced. Weeks two and three added the episode-drop card and the call-ad, pushed 6–8 posts to 46 followers, and reach halved. Posting more to the same tiny audience is suppressing distribution, not expanding it.

**3. The drip isn't dripping — it's dumping, and that's a pipeline bug.** Seven posts went out between 10:28 PM and 10:40 PM on Jul 31. Three more between 12:08 AM and 12:15 AM on Aug 6. Six teasers inside four minutes on Jun 23. When approval lands after `postAt` has already passed, `ig-post.mjs` fires everything due at once — every overdue item in one burst, at whatever hour approval happened. Burst posts average 10.5 reach against 13.0 for slotted ones, and they spend the entire week's feed budget in twelve minutes. **Three of the account's worst-performing posts are midnight dumps.**

**4. The episode-drop card is the weakest thing published, and it's structural.** Avg reach 7.5 — dead last of every content type, below the call-ad and well below story banners. It also carries no venue tag, so it has no reshare path. At 46 followers it's asking people who already saw four story banners this week to click through to a homepage. It's spending a feed slot to say "there is more of this elsewhere."

**5. The only posts that ever earned engagement were the hand-written ones.** Seed and teaser posts averaged 2.8–3.0 likes. Pipeline story banners average 0.8. The teasers were one line and one tag — "A hint of the weekend June 26 – 28. Publishing on Wednesday. @hiwirebrewing_knx". The pipeline captions are 400–600 characters: headline, deck, ★ facts, CTA, tags, hashtags. Longer, more complete, better written — and roughly a quarter of the likes. Completeness may be the problem: a caption that answers everything leaves nothing to save, ask, or tap.

**6. Zero saves is the actionable failure.** Event listings with a date, a time, and an address are the single most save-worthy format on Instagram, and this account has produced 17 of them with zero saves. The ★ facts line — the part someone would actually save — sits fourth in the caption, below a headline and a two-sentence deck, under the "more" fold on most posts.

**7. Best-slot evidence, weak but consistent.** Saturday 9 AM produced the account's best post ever (22). Weekend-morning and early-afternoon slots outperform; the Thu 1 PM / Thu 7 PM pair has now declined two weeks running (16→7 and 15→8). Every one of the ten Thursday posts was a scheduled-day artifact of publish day, not a tested choice.

## 3. Action plan — five experiments

Ordered by expected impact. None are implemented; **all five need your sign-off before the pipeline changes.**

### E1 — Fix the burst (pipeline bug, highest priority)
`ig-post.mjs` should never publish more than one item per run and never more than one per ~4 hours. Overdue items get rescheduled forward into the next open slot rather than dumped. Add a hard quiet window (no posting 10 PM–7 AM) — overdue-at-midnight becomes 8 AM the next morning.
*Measures:* burst count → 0; avg reach of story banners vs. the 13.0 slotted baseline.

### E2 — Cut volume from 6–8 posts a week to 3
Two story banners plus the specials board. Pull the episode-drop card out of the feed entirely (worst reach, no reshare path) and the call-ad down to once a month. Reach per post fell as volume rose; this tests the inverse directly.
*Measures:* avg reach per post, and total weekly reach — the honest test is whether 3 posts beat 8 on *total* reach, not just per-post.

### E3 — Move the slots to where the evidence points
Retire the publish-day Thursday pair. Test **Sat 9 AM** (best slot on record), **Fri 5 PM** (weekend-plans window), and **Wed 12 PM** (episode day, midday). Four weeks fixed, no changes mid-test.
*Measures:* reach by slot against the per-slot table above.

### E4 — Lead the caption with the ★ facts line
Invert the story-banner template for half of all posts: date/time/address first, headline second, deck third or cut. This is a one-function change in `buildCaption()` in `ig-queue.mjs` — the facts are already parsed. A/B by alternating posts.
*Measures:* **saves** (currently 0, the target metric), then likes.

### E5 — Replace the fixed four-hashtag block with a rotating discovery set
The current four tags have produced 8 non-follower accounts in a month. Test 10–12 tags per post drawn from a rotating pool weighted toward discovery volume (`#KnoxvilleTN`, `#ThingsToDoKnoxville`, `#KnoxvilleEvents`, `#VisitKnoxville`, `#UrbanWilderness`, `#KnoxTN`) plus venue- and category-specific tags, alternating against the current block.
*Measures:* non-follower reach via the `follow_type` breakdown — the metric that isolates discovery from the follower feed.

### Standing note for the monthly reviews
Every experiment above moves numbers inside a 46-follower base, where the difference between a hit and a miss is four accounts. They are worth running because they cost nothing and compound later — but **none of them grows the follower base**, and the follower base is the binding constraint. The distribution problem is off-platform: the eight venues already tagged in these posts are the audience-holders in this neighborhood, and not one of them has ever engaged. That's a decision for Andy, not a pipeline setting (see the top of the accompanying message).
