## 2. Findings

**1. Measured at equal age, weekly reach is not falling. It rose.**
Last week's prediction for No. 11 was 8.2 avg; it reads **8.1**. Twelve of its thirteen posts gained and none fell (+22% total). Set against No. 10 *at the same eight-day age*, No. 11 reads **105 against 99** — total reach up 6%, on three more posts. No. 12 at two days reads 7.2 per post against No. 11's 6.6 at one day: also ahead.
So the story the last three reviews led with — a collapse — was the age gap, every time. The accrual curve is now measured across three cohorts: **+24% in week one, +5% in week two, 0% in week three.** A1 is doing its job and should never be relaxed.

**2. Repeating a standing weekly deal with the same caption decays it.**
Earl's specials board has run four weeks with a near-identical caption: **18 → 12 → 6 → 8** (the last is four days old and will accrue). The Playscape story-time banner ran verbatim two weeks running at the same Friday 20:00 slot: **6 → 3**. Standing-deal banners average 9.0 lifetime against 10.9 for one-time events, and No. 12 put three of them in a nine-banner week — the most yet.
n=7 on the standing side, so this is a signal, not a law. But Earl's is a clean single-subject series, and the two worst posts of No. 12 were both repeated standing items. Standing deals are also the posts least connected to "only this weekend matters" — they are true every week, which is exactly why the fourth run of one reads as filler.

**3. The lead cards went out at 1:45 in the morning, and A3 still has not shipped.**
`ig-queue.mjs:201` schedules the promo pair with `isoNowPlus(pr.offset)` — the lead posts fire at **queue-build time**, whatever the clock says. The No. 12 publish ran around 01:30 ET on Wednesday, so the call-the-ear ad, the episode-drop card and the week's lead story (the Vols home opener) all went out between 01:45 and 02:30 AM.
That happens to have satisfied the letter of A3 — nothing posted on Tuesday, for the first time in five weeks — but by accident of when Andy published, not by design. It also means the drip's three lead cards, including the week's biggest story, are handed whatever hour the publish happens to run at. Their reach (9, 5, 9) is not obviously worse than the week's average, so this is a governance problem before it is a performance one. `git log` confirms no commit has touched `ig-queue.mjs`, `ig-schedule.mjs` or `ig-post.mjs` since `993afb9`.

**4. Followers grew 16%; non-follower reach grew 0%.**
74 → 86 followers, and the 30-day non-follower count sits at **10 accounts, 51 views** — 10 and 55 last week. Eleven new posts and twelve new followers bought exactly zero additional strangers. Follower reach did rise (31 → 34), so the new followers *are* being reached; the feed simply has no route past them. The 40% follower-reach rate holds as the list grows, which is the good news: growing the list does grow reach. It is just that the list only grows when Andy grows it. A5 and A6 are still the only two items that touch this.

**5. Follower growth outpaced outbound follows for the first time — one week, small numbers.**
+12 followers against +9 accounts followed. Every prior week ran at about 0.68 followers per follow; this week reads 1.33. Since the baseline the cumulative ratio is now 40/50 = 0.80, up from 0.68. Post-attributed follows still read **zero across all 73 posts**, so nothing in the data says the feed did this. Most likely explanations, in order: lag (people followed back days after being followed), the gameday weekend, or noise on numbers this small. Worth watching for one more week before it means anything.

**6. The bio link got its first click since the caption change shipped.**
`website_clicks` per drip window: 0, 0, 0, 0, **1**. The 30-day total is also 1, meaning the previous lone click has aged out and this is a new one. The captions that shipped in `993afb9` ("Full story at soknoear.com — link in bio") were live for the whole No. 12 window and are the only variable that changed.
One click. It moves A2 off zero and nothing more — but zero-for-four to one-for-one is the difference between a route nobody takes and a route somebody took.

**7. The gameday weekend produced the account's two best days on record.**
Daily account reach hit **13 on Sep 5** and **12 on Sep 2** — first and second in the entire series. Total person-days for the window (46) is still in the same 45–51 band as every other week, so the audience did not grow; it concentrated. The single best *post* of the week was the Saturday-noon Puckers banner (10), the only post tied to the game itself, on the best-performing day of the week and in the account's best-performing day slot.

**8. Still zero comments and zero saves, lifetime, at 73 posts.**
One share, in June. Seven accounts engaged in thirty days. Whatever the reach numbers do week to week, no one has ever saved or replied to anything this account has posted. That is not a caption problem — a save is what someone does with a thing they intend to use on Saturday, which is precisely what these banners are for.

**9. Stories have not moved in a month.**
STORY: 9 unique accounts, 12 views in the last 30 days — identical to last week's pull. Stories are still invisible to `/me/media` and to this review, and nothing has changed about them in a month.
