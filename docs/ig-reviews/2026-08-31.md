## 2. Findings

**1. Post reach accrues ~24% over the week after measurement, and the last review's headline was an artifact of that.**
The ten No. 10 posts read 80 on Aug 24 and 99 today. Every one of them gained: the Thursday 09:00 post went 8 → 11, the Friday carousel 8 → 12, Tuesday 12:45 10 → 12. The Aug 24 review explicitly ruled this out ("about 3%… will not grow into a different story") and then built its lead paragraph on the number. The No. 9 week has drifted 119 → 123 → 128 over three pulls, so the accrual keeps running for weeks, not days.
The practical effect: **every review so far has compared a six-day-old week against a thirteen-day-old one and read the age gap as a decline.** Corrected for age, No. 9 → No. 10 was a 30% drop, not 42%. No. 11 at 6.6 is not comparable to No. 10 at 9.9 at all; at the same accrual rate it lands near 8.2.

**2. Post volume has doubled and total weekly reach has not moved.**
6 → 9 → 10 → 13 posts a week; 63 → 128 → 99 → 86 total weekly reach. There is no positive relationship. Total reach tracks the account's audience (45–52 person-days a week), and the audience is what is flat. Adding posts divides the same reach into more, smaller pieces — which is most of the mechanism behind "per-post reach is falling."
The corollary matters more than the finding: **post volume is a free variable.** It does not buy reach and there is no evidence it costs any either. Set it on editorial grounds, and stop reading per-post averages as a verdict on the content.

**3. Follower growth tracks accounts followed, not anything in the feed.**
Followers +8 this week; accounts followed +13. Since the baseline: followers 46 → 74 (+28), following 51 → 92 (+41). That is 0.68 followers per account followed, holding steady across three weeks whose post metrics looked nothing alike. Post-attributed follows read zero for all 62 posts. Whatever is adding followers is an outbound, manual behaviour — not the drip. Follower count should be read as a measure of Andy's follow activity, not of content performance.

**4. Hashtags now have a control group, and they do nothing.**
Seven captions with no hashtags average 10.7 reach; fifty-two with the standard four average 10.8. Three earlier reviews reported the hashtag block as if it were working; it never had a control, and now that it does, the answer is no effect. Same for @mentions across 62 posts. The four-tag block costs nothing and can stay, but it should never again appear in a finding.

**5. Zero of the four Aug 24 action items shipped.**
`git log` shows no commit touching `ig-queue.mjs`, `ig-schedule.mjs` or `ig-post.mjs` since the review. Concretely: A2's caption line is unchanged — `ig-queue.mjs:86` still emits `Full story in this week's Ear → soknoear.com` and `:189` still emits `Read it — and hear the audio briefing — at soknoear.com`, both unclickable. A3's Wednesday floor was not added, and the drip duly opened Tuesday 16:45 for a Thursday–Sunday episode, the fourth week running. A4's feasibility check is answered below. A5 has now been carried for four reviews.

**6. The location-tag experiment (A4) is not available on this API path.**
`location_id` is documented for media-container creation on `graph.facebook.com` under the Instagram API **with Facebook Login**. It is not documented anywhere in the Instagram-Login publishing guide, which is the path this pipeline uses (`graph.instagram.com`). Proving it definitively would mean firing a real container-create call, which is a write to Instagram and outside this review's remit. Treat location tagging as **unavailable without migrating the app to Facebook Login** — a much larger change than the experiment was worth.
`user_tags` does appear in the Instagram-Login guide (in its URL-encoding examples), which makes tagging the venue **in the image** rather than only in the caption the closest available substitute — and unlike a caption @mention it puts the post in the venue's Tagged tab, which is a discovery surface. Still needs one live container test to confirm.

**7. The week's lead story got the week's third-worst slot performance.**
The 93% lunar eclipse — the largest single item in No. 11 — posted Tuesday 17:30 and drew 5, two days before the event. The three Tuesday posts averaged 6.7 on the weakest day in the record (Tue avg 8.4, n=7, all of it from the last two weeks). The pattern from Finding 5's A3 is unchanged: the drip spends its opening, and its best story, on a day that is outside the weekend the episode covers.

### A1 — Measure each week at equal age *(procedural, no code, no sign-off)*

The just-finished week still gets its post-by-post table, marked provisional, and is never used for a trend claim. Two of the last three reviews' lead paragraphs would not have survived this rule.
*Measures:* every weekly comparison in the next report cites two windows of the same age.

### A2 — Give Instagram a clickable route to the site *(carried, third week — needs Andy)*
Findings 5 and the unmeasured Umami row. Unchanged from Aug 24 and still the highest-value item that is purely mechanical:
- **Andy:** confirm the profile's website field points at `soknoear.com`. Cannot be read from a logged-out fetch.
- **Pipeline, pending your yes:** `ig-queue.mjs:86` → `Full story — link in bio`; `:189` → `Read it and hear the audio briefing — link in bio`. Two string literals, no logic change. I have not touched it, per the standing rule about the posting pipeline.
*Measures:* Instagram-referred sessions in Umami (needs A4), and total sessions on drip days vs non-drip days.

### A3 — Hold the drip inside the episode's own window *(carried; pipeline, low risk — needs Andy)*
Findings 5 and 7. Floor the first scheduled slot in `ig-queue.mjs` at the episode's Wednesday. The promo pair keeps its opening position; it just cannot open two days before the weekend the episode describes. This is the standing "only this weekend matters" rule applied to the drip.
*Measures:* no posts before Wednesday; Tuesday leaves the day table.

### A4 — Provision read-only Umami access for this review *(needs Andy — small)*
Umami went live specifically to answer where the audience comes from, and this review could not read it. Either an Umami API key or a read-only share URL for the soknoear site, dropped into `/var/www/soknoear/.env` as `UMAMI_API_KEY` / `UMAMI_SITE_ID`, makes the A2 measure checkable from here on. Without it, A2 ships blind.
*Measures:* next review contains a referrer table.

### A5 — Replace the location-tag test with an image `user_tags` test *(supersedes Aug 24's A4)*
Finding 6. Location tagging is off the table without a Facebook-Login migration. The substitute: tag the venue **in the image** on half the story banners for three weeks, leaving the caption identical. It is the only lever in this pipeline that puts a post on a surface non-followers see. Sequence: one live container-create test to confirm `user_tags` is accepted on `graph.instagram.com` → then Andy's sign-off → then the A/B.
*Measures:* reach on tagged vs untagged banners, and whether any venue ever engages with a post it was tagged in.

### A6 — Venue outreach *(carried, fourth review — needs Andy)*
Unchanged and still the largest available gain. Nine venues tagged across 62 posts; not one has ever liked, commented on, or reshared. Six of this week's thirteen posts tagged Ijams Park. One reshare from Ijams or Kern's would put the Ear in front of more people than the pipeline has reached in ten weeks. This is a conversation, not a setting.

### Standing note
Ten weeks in: 74 followers, 682 lifetime post-reach, one share, zero comments, zero saves, ~45–52 person-days of reach a week, and no measured traffic to the site. The pipeline ships on schedule every week and the artwork and copy are the best they have been. The constraint has not changed since the first review: **the feed has no route out of its own follower list, and the follower list is growing because Andy follows people, not because the feed reaches anyone new.** A2, A5 and A6 are the only three items here that touch that. A2 has now waited three weeks.
