## 2. Findings

**1. More posts produced more reach, and better reach per post. The "cut to 3–4" recommendation is contradicted.**
Baseline E2 and Aug 13's A1/A3 all argued for cutting weekly volume, on the reading that reach per post fell 16.1 → 10.5 → 10.3 as volume ran 7 → 8 → 6. The No. 9 week ran **nine** posts — the largest week ever — and produced **119 total reach at 13.2 avg**, beating the 6-post week on both axes simultaneously. The decline was not volume-driven; volume and per-post reach moved *together*, not against each other. **A3 (`MAX_POSTS_PER_WEEK = 4`) would have cut this week's reach roughly in half and should not ship as specified.**

**2. The episode-drop card is not the worst content type. That claim was wrong and drove a bad recommendation.**
The Aug 13 review called episode-drop "worst content type on record, avg reach 7.5" and recommended dropping it. Its actual lifetime average is **10.7**, and in the No. 9 week it scored **15 — the second-best post of the week**, beating six of seven story banners. The call-the-ear ad likewise averages 13.0 lifetime and scored 14. Both non-story formats are performing at or above the story banners they were slated to be cut for.

**3. The burst fired exactly as predicted — and its real cost was editorial, not statistical.**
Three posts went out within 23 seconds (7:45:11 / :23 / :34) because approval landed on two overdue items plus episode-drop. But the burst posts averaged 12.3 against 13.7 for the six spaced posts — a real penalty, not a catastrophic one. The genuine damage is narrower and worse: **the hummingbird banner, the week's lead story, drew 8 — the lowest reach of the week** and below the lifetime median, because it was first into a three-post dump at 7:45 AM. The two filler formats behind it got 14 and 15. The burst didn't sink the week; it sank the one post that mattered most.

**4. Followers grew fastest during the heaviest posting week, weakening the "feed doesn't acquire" thesis.**
The Aug 13 review found +3 followers during a six-day blackout (+1.5/day over the final two days) and read it as evidence that growth is entirely off-platform. The No. 9 week produced **+10 in five days (+2.0/day)** — a faster rate, during the account's largest posting week. Post-attributed follows remain zero, but that field has never been non-zero for any post and looks inert on this token rather than informative. Two data points don't settle it, but the blackout finding no longer supports the conclusion drawn from it. **A5 (get one off-platform attribution number) stays the right call** — it is still the only thing that would actually resolve this.

**5. Engagement is still essentially nil, and that hasn't moved.**
One share in 39 posts, zero comments, zero saves, ever. Engagement rate this week was 9.2% of reach (10 likes + 1 share / 119) against 10.9% lifetime — flat. Reach is growing; interaction is not following it. None of the eight tagged venues has ever engaged. This is the finding that has survived all three reviews unchanged, and it is the one the pipeline cannot fix.

**6. No pipeline code shipped; No. 10 has no queue yet.**
E1–E5 remain unimplemented — no commits touched `ig-queue.mjs`, `ig-post.mjs`, or `ig-approve.mjs`. Vol 1 No. 10 (Aug 20–23) published on the site, but `content/ig-queue/` on the VPS holds nothing past `2026-08-13.json`. That is on schedule — every prior queue was staged the morning of the episode drop — so **the No. 10 queue is due Thursday Aug 20** and is an open ask, not a failure.

## 3. Action plan

### A1 — Ship the burst guard, scoped down to ordering *(needs sign-off)*
Finding 3 says the burst's cost is concentrated in one place: the lead story landing in a dump. The fix does not need the full E1. Two changes to `ig-post.mjs`/`ig-queue.mjs`:
- **One post per cron run** (the account posts 9/week; a `*/15` cron has ~670 slots — there is no reason to ever send two at once).
- **Overdue items reschedule forward** to the next free slot instead of firing immediately on approval.

Skip the 4-hour minimum spacing and the quiet window for now — Thu 9/10/13 spacing at 1-hour gaps produced 17/16/17, the three best posts of the week, so wide spacing is not supported by the data.
*Measures:* burst count → 0; lead-story reach vs. the 12.9 story-banner average.

### A2 — Withdraw A3 (the volume cap) and keep staging every story *(no code change)*
Finding 1 kills it. Nine posts beat six on total reach *and* per-post reach. Leave `ig-queue.mjs` staging one post per story. If a cap is ever added it should be a floor-protection device, not a limit — revisit only if a week ever shows per-post reach falling *while* total reach falls too, which has not happened.

### A3 — Give the lead story the 9 AM Thursday slot *(pipeline change, low risk)*
The two best slots on record are Thu 9:00 (19.5 avg, n=2) and the 9/10/13 Thursday band generally (17/16/17 this week). The lead story currently gets whatever slot the queue builder assigns first, which this week was the 7:45 dump. Have the builder pin the week's lead story to **Thursday 9:00 AM** and push the episode-drop and call-the-ear cards to later slots — they demonstrably don't need the prime one (14 and 15 from the worst slot of the week).
*Measures:* lead-story reach ≥ 17 next week.

### A4 — Stop quoting the aggregate account metric; use the day series
The `days_28`/`week` `total_value` reach endpoint returns 4 against a daily series summing far higher. Update the review procedure to pull `period=day` with explicit `since`/`until` and sum it. This also retires the "31 unique accounts / 8 non-followers" figure the last two reviews leaned on — that number was never sound.

### A5 — Get one off-platform attribution number *(needs Andy — carried forward)*
Unchanged from the Aug 13 review and still the highest-value unknown, though Finding 4 has flipped which way it might resolve. Post-attributed follows read zero for all 39 posts including the +10 week, so the field is inert, not informative. If the site logs referrers or the newsletter reports click-through: **where did the last ten followers come from?** One number decides whether the feed is the growth engine or a courtesy channel.

### Standing note
Reach is now growing and volume is not the constraint. Interaction is: one share and zero comments across 39 posts, and no tagged venue has ever engaged. The next real gain is a venue resharing an Ear post to an audience that isn't already following — and that is an outreach decision for Andy, not a pipeline setting.
