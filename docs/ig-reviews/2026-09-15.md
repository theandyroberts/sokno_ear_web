## 2. Findings

**1. The account's best recurring post failed silently, for the second time, and nothing noticed.**
`call-the-ear` was due Wednesday 17:00. `ig-post.mjs` created the container, polled until `status_code` came back **`FINISHED`**, then called `media_publish` — which returned **9007 / subcode 2207027, "The media is not ready for publishing."** The post was stamped `failed` and left there. It is still `failed`. The identical failure hit `2026-08-26/opera-quarry` on Aug 26 and was never mentioned in any review.

Three separate defects, in order of cost:

- **`FINISHED` is not a publish guarantee.** The poll at `scripts/ig-post.mjs:75-81` does exactly what `0f3d768` intended and it is still not enough — Meta returns `FINISHED` and then rejects the publish. The publish call itself needs the retry, not just the container.
- **Nothing retries.** `post.status = "failed"` (`ig-post.mjs:97`) is terminal. The cron re-runs every 15 minutes and skips anything not `due`, so a post that fails once is gone for good — even though the failure is explicitly transient and says "please wait for a moment."
- **Nothing alerts.** The script `process.exit(1)`s into a log file. `scripts/alert.mjs` exists and is already wired into `healthcheck.sh`; the poster does not use it. Two failed posts in three weeks reached nobody.

On its lifetime average (11.5) the missing ad is worth about 22% of the week's total reach.

**2. Weekly reach halved because the week was half as long, not because posts got worse.**
84 → 38. But No. 13 ran **six posts against eleven**: the episode carried a feature and **four** stories (No. 12 had a feature and eight; No. 11 had eleven), and the seventh queued post failed. Per post, No. 13 reads **6.3 at age 3** against No. 12's 7.2 at age 2 and No. 11's 6.6 at age 1 — the same band. Person-days tell the same story: 23 against 46, with the peak day at 7 against 13.
This is an editorial-input number, not a pipeline number. The drip published everything it was given, on time, in the right window.

**3. The accrual curve published last week was wrong, and its projection missed by 14%.**
Last week's rule was +24% / +5% / 0%, and it projected No. 12 to "near 98." No. 12 reads **84** at age 10 and is nearly done accruing. The +24% came from cohorts first measured at **age 1** (No. 10, No. 11); No. 12 was first measured at **age 2** and gained **+6%**.
Corrected rule: **a cohort measured at age 0–1 is being read mid-count and will rise 20–25%. A cohort measured at age ≥2 is within ~6% of final, and within ~1% by age 8.** No. 13, measured at age 3, should mature near **40**. The same caution applies to this week's like-rate.

**4. Followers +10%, non-follower reach −40%, and the follower-reach rate broke.**
86 → 95 followers. 28-day unique reach 44 → **35**; non-follower **10 → 6**; non-follower share 23% → **17%**. The feed reached **29 of 95 followers — 31%**, against 40% and 42% the two weeks before.
Last week's review asserted the 40% rate "holds as the list grows," and treated that as the good news. It does not hold. Adjusting for the shorter week does not rescue it either: over the 28-day frame, post count fell **7%** while unique reach fell **20%** and non-follower reach fell **40%**. Nine new followers this week bought no additional reach at all.

**5. Follower growth outpaced outbound follows for a second week.**
+9 followers against +7 follows, after +12 against +9. Cumulative since baseline: 49 gained on 57 follows, **0.86**, up from 0.80 and 0.68. Two weeks is not a trend, and post-attributed follows still read **zero across all 79 posts** — nothing in the data says the feed did this. But it is now the second week, and the ratio has risen three reviews running.

**6. Instagram sends the site about one visit a week, and has done so since before the caption change.**
A4 is closed and it answers A2. `l.instagram.com`: **0, 1, 1, 1** across the No. 10–No. 13 windows. The caption line shipped `993afb9` on **Sep 1** — live for No. 12 and No. 13 only. The No. 11 window, which predates it, also reads 1.
Last week's Finding 6 ("the bio link got its first click") was built on `website_clicks` moving 0 → 1. That same field has since restated No. 7 from 0 back to 1, six weeks after the fact. **The route exists, it delivers roughly one visit a week, and nothing the pipeline has changed has moved it.** For scale: the site took **334 visits** in 30 days and Instagram supplied **3** — under 1%, against 123 pageviews on `/dirtysouthparty` alone.

**7. A3 passed its measures, but handed the lead cards the second-worst slot on record.**
First clean week: nothing before Wednesday, nothing outside 08:00–20:00, no Tuesday, no 02:00. The fix works.
What it does not do is aim. `isoPromoSlot` (`scripts/ig-queue.mjs:196-201`) takes `Math.max(Date.now(), publishDay 09:00)` — a floor with no ceiling. Andy published at **16:55**, so the floor was already passed and the promo pair inherited the clock exactly as before, landing at 17:00 / 17:20 / 17:40. **17:00 is n=8, avg 7.8 — the second-worst hour on record**, and every cell in the 09:00–13:00 band A3 was written to reach sits above it. The episode-drop card at 17:30 read 5, the joint-worst post of the week; the Thursday 09:00 Kern's card read 6.
Small numbers, and the lead cards are not obviously the problem. But the mechanism A3 was meant to install — *lead cards get a deliberate slot* — is still not installed. It is a one-line change to the same function.

**8. A7(a) works, and a renamed story id walked straight through it.**
`ig-repeats.mjs` held out Earl's and Hi-Wire exactly as designed. But the Playscape story time ran a **third consecutive Friday** — Aug 28 (6), Sep 4 (3), Sep 11 (5) — because it arrived as **`playscape-fall`** where the first two runs were **`playscape-storytelling`**. Detection is by exact story id (`priorRunsById`), so a rename is a clean bypass, and the module's own docstring names the Playscape banner as one of the three cases it exists to stop.
Not malice and not a bug in the logic — it is that story ids are editorial, hand-written, and change when the angle changes. The cap needs a second signal (venue + recurrence, or banner-line similarity) or an explicit `standing: true` flag on the story.

**9. Ijams is now a quarter of everything posted, at the worst reach of any venue.**
Three of No. 13's five banners tagged `@ijamsnaturecenter`; the tag is 14 posts since Aug 11 at **7.8 avg**, below the 9.8 untagged baseline and below every venue with more than one post. Ijams has never liked, commented on or reshared anything. The concentration is an editorial-sourcing fact — Ijams publishes a lot of events — but the feed is spending its best slots on the venue that returns least.

**10. Still zero comments and zero saves, lifetime, at 79 posts.**
One share, in June. Four accounts engaged in 28 days, down from seven. Post-attributed profile visits did not move at all this week (21 → 21). Twelve weeks.

### A8 — Make a failed post loud, and retry it *(new — the week's only blocking item, needs Andy)*
Finding 1. Two silent, permanent losses in three weeks, both the same transient error, both after the container reported `FINISHED`. Three changes, all in `scripts/ig-post.mjs`, all small:

1. **Retry the publish, not just the container.** On `9007` / subcode `2207027`, sleep and re-issue `media_publish` against the same `creation_id` — the container is valid for 24h. Three attempts at 30s / 2m / 10m covers a transient by a wide margin.
