## 2. Findings

**1. The week was carried by four venues that passed their post on.** 926 reach is not seventeen posts doing better; it is thirteen posts doing what they always do (9–38) and four doing something the account had never seen (103–286). Each of the four was a bar or brewery new to the feed, tagged in the image, and emailed that morning; each registered exactly the shares (1–2) a reshare to the venue's own story would leave; each brought profile visits (2–7) and, for the first time ever, follows (1–4). The three other new, written-to, tagged venues — Trailhead, The 808, Old Sevier Market — read 9–38. So the pipeline's job is to give every post the best chance of being passed on, and this week says what that chance is made of: the right tag, on the right day, with the venue told it's coming.

**2. Followers +45.** Half of it (21) came before the drip, alongside 34 outbound follows, the engine the account has always run on. The other half came with the drip, and seven of those are the first follows the API has ever pinned to a post. Followers who saw a post: 23%. The number to watch now is not followers but whether the account's reach stays above its follower count when the venues don't reshare.

**3. Every change shipped Oct 5 did what it was meant to.** Tag every post: 14 of 14 tagged, non-follower reach 1 → 660. Singles back: 17 posts, lowest 9 (lowest was 4). The roundup: 14 (needed 6). The measures in `docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md` item 2 are all cleared; that item closes.

**4. The old posts grew with the new ones.** No. 16 doubled at age 7–12 because 51 people visited the profile and tapped back through the grid. A quiet week's posts stop growing after two weeks; a loud week lifts everything behind it. The reach-per-post line on the dashboard is still read at equal age, which is the only fair reading; but "posts earn reach for two weeks" is now "posts earn reach as long as something sends people to the profile."

**5. Tag the people, not just the room.** Andy's note on Friday ("tags the venue and artist") and finding 1 say the same thing: what reached people was an account with its own crowd being tagged in the picture. A band, a DJ, a comic has a crowd too, and Feral Kitty, Dizzle Phunk, Daniel Kimbro, the six After Brunch comics, the Scruffy Roots acts all have handles the drip never carried. Shipped (below), on Wednesday unless Andy says don't.

**6. The ad is held.** Andy is changing the phone-in system; the "Ear sees the future" card goes out of the No. 18 drip and stays out until he marks the hold done. It read 14 this week, 11.2 lifetime, so the drip loses nothing while it's out, and Wednesday morning loses the three-posts-in-45-minutes cluster the ad made.

**7. The Reel exists and publishes.** Built, committed, proved against the live API (container `FINISHED` in 30 s, never published), and in the queue builder for Wednesday at 1 PM. It is the first format on this account that Instagram distributes to non-followers on its own, which this week shows is where the audience is.

**8. Zero saves, still.** 131 posts. The roundup's "save the one you want" has had none. Shares are the engagement the account gets (10 lifetime, 8 this week), and shares are what moved the needle. Saves stop being a measure here; shares and follows per post take their place.

### A weekly Reel from the audio briefing *(built this run, recut Tue Oct 13 at Andy's note, first post Wed Oct 14 at 1 PM, move `weekly-reel`)*
Finding 7. The first cut (Mon) was the briefing's open and feature paragraph, 42 s over one banner. Andy, Tue 00:40: "static and boring. I'd love it to be a quick quip about each of the featured articles/events & have the screen change to show the event artwork + info for each one." Recut that night. Three pieces:

- **`scripts/ig-reel.py`** cuts `public/assets/ig/<slug>/reel.mp4` + `reel.jpg` as a montage: the episode card with a one-line intro, then every story in the episode's order — its banner pushing in slowly (6% over the segment), the When and Where facts under it, the quip spoken and burned in, a "5 of 16" counter in the masthead — then the card and a sign-off. The quips come from `content/reel-scripts/<slug>.json` (`intro`, `outro`, `quips` by story id), then the story's `social.igQuip`, then the banner's two lines. Each quip is its own ElevenLabs call (the briefing's voice `fnpVoGi5UsfmnuvEQTHG`, `eleven_v3`, the briefing's settings; the LEXICON's TTS respellings are parsed from the table and applied to the speech, the caption keeps the real spelling), cached by text in `data/reel-tts/` so a rebuild only pays for changed lines; one call per line means every segment's length is exact and there is no pause-hunting. Frames are Pillow, piped raw into ffmpeg; the audio is laid with `adelay` + `amix`. The key is `$ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` or the keychain item the ElevenLabs MCP reads. No. 17: 16 stories, 98.8 s, 2,963 frames, about a minute to build (20 s of it the voice). Default cap 120 s so a 16-story week keeps every story; `--max` lowers it; `--plan` prints the cut without rendering or paying.
- **`scripts/ig-container.mjs`** publishes a `media_type=REELS` container (`video_url`, `cover_url`, `share_to_feed`) and gives it the video wait: 36 polls at 10 s (six minutes) against the image's ten at 4 s; `status` is read with `status_code` so an `ERROR` says why. `kind: "reel"` in the result. Proved Monday against the live API with the first cut: container created, `IN_PROGRESS` for 30 s, `FINISHED`, never published, left to expire.
- **`scripts/ig-reel.mjs`** is the queue entry (`id: "reel"`, publish day 13:00, undated; the caption says how many things and how many seconds, from the `reel.json` the cutter writes) and `reelIsOn(moves)`; `ig-queue.mjs` adds it when `reel.mp4` exists, Andy hasn't said "Don't", and the week hasn't already gone out without one. `publish-episode.mjs` runs the cutter after the promos, and a week where it fails still publishes. The desk shows it as `ig-post:reel`.

The Tuesday draft task writes the quips (step 3a) next to the audio script; `content/reel-scripts/2026-10-07.json` is the model and `2026-10-14.json` is written for No. 18 (18 stories). `social.igQuip` is in the schema as the in-episode alternative. Nine tests in `tests/ig-reel.test.ts`; the suite is 317 passing. `soknoear.com` serves the mp4 with `Accept-Ranges: bytes` (checked), which Instagram's fetch needs.

*Measures, Oct 19:* the Reel posted (the desk box); its reach, views and follows against the week's stills; how far people watch (the API's Reel metrics, if it returns them); whether `plays` is among them.
